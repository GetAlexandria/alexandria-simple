The Technical Overlay

← Research Playbook

Why "Technical" is a concentrated overlay on a shared product core — not a separate job family — and where that overlay changes the job versus where it barely touches it.

The Core Claim

Every PM has the same eight jobs. What they don't all have is the same depth in each. "Technical" is not a modifier that replaces some jobs with others — it is an additive layer that concentrates depth in a specific subset while leaving the rest of the job unchanged.

The result: Technical Product = Base Product + the teal layer. Nothing is removed. The technical plays are additions, not substitutions.
The key test: if you stripped the technical overlay from a Technical PM, would you have a non-technical PM? Yes — the base plays remain intact. The overlay is purely additive. This is why the two-color card system in the playbook works: every base card (dashed gold border) is also present in a "Base PM" role.

Where It Concentrates

The technical overlay is not uniformly distributed across the eight jobs. It concentrates heavily in three and is nearly absent in two.
JobOverlay densityTechnical plays addedWhy
📐 Product Definition Very high 4 plays (all 3 tiers) The spec is where technical credibility directly translates into engineering trust.
🛠️ Delivery & Execution High 3 plays (Coord + PM) Build sequencing, backlog health, and anti-pattern detection require system knowledge.
🔍 Customer & Market Insight Medium 3 plays (all 3 tiers) Feasibility and technical risk assessment happen during discovery, not later.
🎯 Strategy & Prioritization Medium 2 plays (Sr. only) At senior scope: build-vs-buy and technical standards become strategic calls.
⚙️ Product Operations Low 1 play (Coord only) Dev toolchain management is the one operational surface that changes.
📊 Analytics & Outcomes Low 1 play (PM only) Instrumentation during build is the main technical contribution here.
🚀 Launch & Go-to-Market Absent 0 plays Launch is coordination-heavy — technical depth doesn't change the shape of this job.
📣 Stakeholder Communication Absent 0 plays Communication craft is durable — technical expertise informs content, not the job itself.

The Two-Layer Architecture

Base product core
54 plays shared across the product job family. Every PM — regardless of technical depth — owns these. They represent the durable job of product management: discover, decide, build, ship, measure, communicate.
Technical overlay
13 plays added when the role is specifically "Technical." They require system knowledge, architecture fluency, or engineering judgment. An agent can stack them on top of the base; a human trades breadth for that depth.

The Human Nuance

For a human PM, "Technical" implies a trade-off. Gaining depth in the technical overlay typically means having less bandwidth for something else — often breadth of feature ownership, or the communication and narrative work that can feel softer.
For an agent, the trade-off disappears. An agent can stack every overlay because it doesn't have a bandwidth ceiling. The playbook is designed for both audiences: the technical overlay cards represent genuine additional work that a human has to budget for, but that an agent model can treat as simply additive. This is one of the design reasons for making the overlay explicit and separated — it surfaces what is genuinely different about this role variant, without hiding it inside a single monolithic job description.

What This Model Enables

Future lens: company stage. The next planned dimension is a company-stage modifier — live-prototype → building → scaling. Different stages emphasize different jobs. At live-prototype, Insight and Definition dominate. At scaling, Analytics, Strategy, and Communication take over. The overlay model composes: Technical × Stage would produce a matrix of play sets for each combination.
