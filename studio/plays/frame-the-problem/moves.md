### pre_fill

Backstage drafts a first framing for Raven to react to — never a blank question.

- Reads what the director handed in: a solution he's holding, a rough problem statement, a conversation, notes.
- Frames the problem(s) underneath it — who has each, the circumstance where it bites, the evidence — in service of facts and logic, not the solution, staying genuinely open about whether the framing supports, complicates, or undercuts what he brought.
- Marks every piece of evidence honestly (`first-hand` / `assumed / hand-wavey` / `hypothetical`) and names the thin spot still needing a specific past instance.
- Hands Raven her marching orders: what to put in front of him, and the thin spot to draw out — carrying the open evidence bar ("Can you tell me about a specific time that happened recently?").

**↳ Nothing framable — say so, don't invent.**
If the handed-in material has nothing framable in it — logistics, chatter, empty — backstage writes that plainly in the framing doc and tells Raven to check whether he meant to hand over something else. It never manufactures a problem to be helpful.

### review

The human gate: the director reacts to the draft through Raven. This is the one switchback in the play.

- Raven puts the current draft in front of the director.
- He reacts — gives feedback to fold in, or approves to finish.
- His reaction is what the next move works from.

**↳ Approve — the framing is done.**
When the director approves, the loop ends and the play exits. The co-edited document stands as the deliverable — no spoken read-back, no second rendering.

**↳ Revise — his reaction goes backstage.**
When he gives feedback instead of approving, his reaction travels to `revise`,
where backstage folds it in and rewrites the document whole: reads the current
draft, his latest reaction, and the original material; keeps what he confirmed,
changes what he corrected, adds the evidence he gave, and re-marks everything
honestly (a specific recent time is real; hand-wavey still isn't); bumps the
version. It holds the line — support for the solution is earned only by a
specific past instance, never granted to be agreeable; a reaction that
contradicts something he said earlier surfaces as a tension rather than getting
silently resolved; an argument for the solution with no real instance gets noted
as still needing one. The updated draft comes back to him, and the loop runs as
many times as the editing takes. When nothing real is still open, backstage
signals it reads as ready — but the document always returns to `review`, because
approval is his to give, never the play's to assume.
