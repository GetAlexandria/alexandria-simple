# Problem Framing — Account managers have no renewal visibility in Meridian
status: draft (v1)

## The problem(s)

### Account managers are flying blind on renewals

- **Who has it:** Account managers (AMs) using Meridian to manage their book of business. It bites when a renewal date is approaching and the AM has no system-generated signal — they only discover it by accident or through personal workarounds.
- **Evidence:**
  - "I went through my book last week and realized I had three accounts up for renewal in the next thirty days that I had no idea about." — Clara. `first-hand: a specific past instance` (last week, her own book, three accounts, thirty-day window)
  - "I only found out because I happened to open the account record for a completely different reason." — Clara. `first-hand: a specific past instance` (same incident — discovery was accidental)
  - "One of them was sixty thousand ARR." — Clara. `first-hand: a specific past instance` (quantifies the exposure on one of the three accounts)
  - "If I hadn't clicked in that day those accounts would have hit the end of their term with zero outreach from us." — Clara. `first-hand: a specific past instance` (same incident — describes what would have happened without the accident)
  - "There is nothing in Meridian that tells you 'hey, this one's expiring.'" — Clara. `assumed / hand-wavey` (stated as a general claim about the product, not tied to a specific investigation of Meridian's feature set or a documented gap)
  - "I've heard it from a few other AMs too. Not just you." — Prakash. `assumed / hand-wavey` (secondhand, no names, no specific instances cited)
- **Thin spot:** Prakash's claim that other AMs experience this too. Clara's instance is strong, but whether this is a team-wide pattern or concentrated in her workflow still rests on "I've heard it from a few other AMs" — no specific second instance from a second person yet.

### AMs are papering over the gap with personal calendar hacks

- **Who has it:** The same AM population, in the same renewal-tracking circumstance. It bites because the workaround is fragile, duplicative, and invisible to the org — if an AM leaves or their personal calendar diverges from the system of record, the workaround breaks silently.
- **Evidence:**
  - "I have reminders set in my personal calendar because Meridian has nothing." — Clara. `first-hand: a specific past instance` (Clara's own current practice)
  - "Half the team is doing the same." — Clara. `assumed / hand-wavey` (no names, no specific other AM's workaround described)
  - "So we're papering over a product hole with personal calendar hacks." — Clara. `first-hand: a specific past instance` (Clara's own characterization of her own practice, which she has direct knowledge of — but the "we" framing extends it to the team without evidence)
- **Thin spot:** The claim that "half the team" shares this workaround. Clara can speak for herself — the team-wide extent is hand-wavey until at least one other AM confirms they do the same thing.

## How they relate (a guess)

These are not two independent problems — the second (calendar hacks) is a coping behavior driven by the first (no renewal visibility). If Meridian surfaced upcoming renewals, the personal-calendar workaround would likely disappear. Held loosely: it is possible the workaround also compensates for other Meridian gaps (e.g., missing task management, weak notification infrastructure), but the material only connects it to renewal visibility.

## What this means for the solution (so far)

**Supports** the idea that Meridian needs some form of proactive renewal surfacing — Clara's first-hand instance is concrete and recent, and the financial exposure ($60K ARR on one account alone) makes the stakes legible. **Complicates** any specific solution scoping, because the team-wide extent is still hand-wavey: if this is primarily Clara's workflow gap, the right intervention might be different from one designed for the whole AM team. The material does not yet earn a claim about frequency, volume, or total ARR at risk across the book.
