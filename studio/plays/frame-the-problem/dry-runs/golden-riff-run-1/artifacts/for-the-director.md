# For the Director — Raven's marching orders

## What to put in front of him

Hand him the problem framing as-is. Two problems surfaced, not one — and he
should see them separated because they need different evidence and may need
different solutions. The framing is a draft; his job is to react to it and
sharpen it, not approve it.

## What to draw out

### Thin spot 1: the tab-loss problem is real but un-pinned

Maya's pattern is corroborated ("I've watched you do it") but there's no single
specific instance on the table yet. The bar: one real, recent time she lost
source material in tabs and it never made it into the library.

**The move:** "Maya described losing source material in tabs — can you tell me
about a specific time that happened recently? Which piece of material was it, and
what were you trying to capture?"

He doesn't need the exact date — he needs one real instance so the problem
carries weight beyond "it happens all the time."

### Thin spot 2: the duplicate-addition problem is entirely hand-wavey

Maya said directors "keep telling us" they re-add things, but no specific
director, no specific duplicate, no specific occasion was cited. This claim is
not yet evidence. The bar: one real instance of a director adding material that
was already in the library, and what happened as a result.

**The move:** "The framing also pulled out a second problem — directors re-adding
material that's already banked. Can you think of a specific time that happened?
Which director, which piece of material?"

If he can't produce one, that problem gets downgraded or dropped — it may be a
fear rather than a fact. That's fine; the framing should reflect what's real.
