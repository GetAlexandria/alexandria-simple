# Refusal Report

## What I received

A 28-line transcript between Sam, Priya, and Leo, ending with Leo saying "Raven, frame that." The invocation moment is marked at line 27.

## What the transcript contains

The entire conversation is logistics and coordination:

- **Ownership assignment** — who drives the onboarding checklist redesign for Q3 (lines 1-7).
- **Meeting scheduling** — when to loop in product, Thursday morning vs. afternoon (lines 9-13).
- **Scope negotiation** — whether the help article update belongs in this scope or a separate ticket (lines 15-19).
- **Rollout sequencing** — ship first, outreach in Q4 (lines 21-23).

## Why this work cannot run

There is no problem discussion in this transcript. No one describes a user pain point, a broken workflow, a technical limitation, a product gap, or any substantive "what's wrong and why" reasoning. The conversation is entirely about who will do the work, when they will meet, and what is in or out of scope — coordination mechanics, not problem framing.

"Frame that" points at a scheduling and ownership thread. A problem brief requires claims about a problem: who is affected, what breaks, what the evidence is. This transcript supplies none of that. Framing it would mean inventing a problem statement from thin air, which is the opposite of verbatim-traceable analysis.

## Recommendation

If the intent was to frame the problem behind the onboarding checklist redesign, that discussion needs to happen first — the "why redesign" conversation — and then the framing invocation can fire on a transcript that contains it.
