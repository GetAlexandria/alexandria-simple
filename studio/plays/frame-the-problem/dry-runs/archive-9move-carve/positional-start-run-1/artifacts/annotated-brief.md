## Problem 1 — "I have no way of knowing a renewal is coming until I stumble into it"

checks: pass

**Progress sought:** Stay ahead of every renewal in my book so I can begin outreach before the term expires — not discover it by accident when it may already be too late.

**Who:** Account Manager (AM). Clara is the speaker; Prakash corroborates hearing the same from other AMs.

**Circumstance:** An AM is working their book day-to-day — logging activity, reviewing accounts, planning follow-up actions. At no point does Meridian surface the fact that a renewal date is approaching. The AM only discovers an impending renewal if they happen to open that specific account record for an unrelated reason. With dozens of accounts, manually auditing every record every day is not realistic, so renewals approach silently until one is stumbled upon or missed entirely.

**Evidence:**

1. "Our account managers are flying blind on renewals." — Clara · `opinion` · conviction high
2. "I went through my book last week and realized I had three accounts up for renewal in the next thirty days that I had no idea about." — Clara · `specific-past`
3. "I only found out because I happened to open the account record for a completely different reason." — Clara · `specific-past`
4. "There is nothing in Meridian that tells you 'hey, this one's expiring.'" — Clara · `opinion` · conviction high
5. "By accident." — Clara · `specific-past`
6. "If I hadn't clicked in that day those accounts would have hit the end of their term with zero outreach from us." — Clara · `hypothetical-future` · conviction high
7. "One of them was sixty thousand ARR." — Clara · `specific-past`
8. "That's not a tracking failure, that's a product gap." — Clara · `opinion` · conviction high
9. "Yeah, that's a real thing." — Prakash · `opinion` · conviction high
10. "I've heard it from a few other AMs too. Not just you." — Prakash · `specific-past`
11. "I have reminders set in my personal calendar" — Clara · `commitment` — ongoing effort invested to build and maintain a manual workaround outside the product
12. "because Meridian has nothing" — Clara · `opinion` · conviction high (second clause of the same sentence as evidence 11)
13. "Half the team is doing the same." — Clara · `opinion` · conviction moderate — round number suggests estimation; no named corroboration beyond Prakash's general confirmation
14. "So we're papering over a product hole with personal calendar hacks." — Clara · `opinion` · conviction high

**What it's not:** A request for a renewal dashboard, a reminder system, or any particular notification mechanism. The problem is that the AM has no signal — from any surface in Meridian — that a renewal is approaching. The conversation names personal-calendar reminders as the current workaround, not as the desired end state.

**Where it lands:** Renewal tracking (no dedicated surface exists today — the surface map confirms that renewal dates live as a field on the account record with no dashboard, alert, or list view). Also touches the book-of-business view (no way to filter or sort by upcoming renewal) and the scheduling & calendar integration surface (one-directional sync cannot push reminders back to an AM's calendar).

**Insight:** The strongest evidence is not Clara's opinion about the product gap — it is the shape of the anecdote: three renewals within thirty days, discovered by accident, one worth sixty thousand ARR. That sequence shows a real near-miss whose cost was only avoided by luck, not by process. The personal-calendar workaround (commitment-grade evidence) confirms that AMs already know the signal is missing and are spending their own effort to compensate for it.
