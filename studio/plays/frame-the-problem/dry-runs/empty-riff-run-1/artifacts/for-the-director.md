# For Raven — next exchange

## What to put in front of him

Show him the framing doc as-is. The honest read: what he handed you is a solution with enthusiasm behind it but no problem underneath it yet. That doesn't mean the idea is bad — it means we don't have anything to frame.

## What to draw out

There's one question worth asking, and it's genuinely open-ended:

> "I like the energy — but help me find the problem this solves. Is there something about how Lantern feels to users right now that's not working? Can you tell me about a specific time a user — or you — felt like the product was missing something in that direction?"

If he gives a hand-wavey answer ("it just feels sterile," "people want personality"), the follow-up is:

> "Can you tell me about a specific time that happened recently? One real moment where that gap showed up."

If he can't find one, tell him plainly: this might be a fun idea worth doing for its own sake, but it's not something the framing play can help with — there's no problem to frame. He may want to hand you something else, or he may just want to ship the owl because he likes it. Either is fine; we just shouldn't pretend there's a problem-framing exercise behind it.
