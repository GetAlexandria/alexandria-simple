# For the Director — Raven's marching orders

## What to put in front of him
Show him the framing as-is. The two problems are clear and he'll recognize them — capture friction and save reliability. The interesting part is how thin the evidence is under both. Let him read it; don't summarize.

## What to draw out

### Thin spot 1: Capture friction scope — only one data point (Tariq's cousin)
**Evidence bar:** A specific, recent time a real user hit this friction — not "it happens all the time" but "last week, user X tried to capture a note on the bus and gave up because..."
**The move:** The cousin story is a real instance, but it's one person relayed second-hand. Ask: "Can you tell me about another specific time you saw or heard about someone hitting this — recently, with Zola?" If he gives a hand-wavey answer ("I'm sure tons of people feel this way"), the follow-up is simply: "Can you tell me about a specific time that happened recently?"

### Thin spot 2: What exactly causes the friction — keyboard, taps, or lock-screen?
**Evidence bar:** One specific instance where someone can say which step in the flow was the wall — "I was trying to jot a thought and the problem was specifically [X]."
**The move:** Ask: "When Tariq's cousin said 'typing in the moment felt too fiddly' — do we know what part she meant? The keyboard itself, the steps to get to a new note, or something else?" This may need to go back to the cousin or to other users. If he doesn't have it, note it as something to find out — don't push him to guess.

### Thin spot 3: Save reliability — entirely third-hand, no specific instance
**Evidence bar:** One concrete case: who lost a note, when, what they were doing.
**The move:** Ask: "Lena mentioned Kenji is hearing about lost notes from users — has anyone gotten a specific story? Like, 'I wrote X on Tuesday and it was gone when I came back'?" If not, suggest Kenji is the trail: he's already in those conversations and could surface a real instance quickly.

## General posture
He's going to want to jump to the lock-screen shortcut (Tariq already floated it). That's fine to note as a hypothesis, but the framing says we don't yet know if that's the right fix because nobody has pinned the friction to the lack of a lock-screen entry point specifically. Don't fight the solution — just keep asking for the instance that earns it.
