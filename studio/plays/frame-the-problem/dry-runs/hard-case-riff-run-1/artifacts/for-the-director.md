# For the Director — pre_fill marching orders

## What to put in front of him

Hand him `problem-framing.md` as-is. Tell him: "This pulls out five distinct problems from the conversation. Two of them have solid evidence — Theo's half-day rewrite and your staffing miss on Ops. The other three each have one instance or are still partly assumption. I want to make sure the framing is right before we go anywhere near solutions."

Let him react. He'll likely correct, merge, or reorder. That's the point.

## What to draw out

Two thin spots need specific past instances before they can carry weight:

### 1. Is the approval bottleneck more than Bex's one bad week?
- **Evidence bar:** One specific instance beyond Bex's (e.g., another contributor whose drafts sat, or another director who experienced the same queue). Bex claims "every director hates it" but that's hand-wavey until someone else's story is on the table.
- **The move:** "Bex described the week you were out — eleven drafts stuck, two sources went stale. Has that happened to anyone else on the team, or was that a one-off because of timing?" If he gives a hand-wavey answer ("oh it happens all the time"), follow up: "Can you tell me about a specific time that happened recently?"

### 2. Are duplicate cards actually widespread, or a few isolated cases?
- **Evidence bar:** One more specific instance of cross-area duplication beyond Priya's board deck, ideally with a concrete consequence (a wrong decision, wasted work, a misleading metric).
- **The move:** "Nadia mentioned Priya finding the same board deck in two areas. Has anyone else hit that — where a duplicate card led to a bad call or wasted effort?" If he says "we've got dupes everywhere" (Roman's line), the follow-up is: "Can you tell me about a specific time a duplicate actually caused a problem?"

### Secondary (if there's time):
- **Atomization:** Sam's twelve-vs-three is vivid but alone. "Has anyone else hit a case where the same kind of document atomized into a wildly different number of cards?" One more instance makes it a pattern; without it, it might be a quirk.
- **Onboarding:** Sam's experience is concrete but it's one person. "When the last person before Sam joined, was their ramp-up similar, or was Sam's situation unusual?"

## What NOT to do
- Don't bring up solutions yet. The framing has to be right first — Nadia and Theo both said the team keeps jumping to features.
- Don't challenge Roman's auto-approver directly. It's parked, not rejected. If the approval bottleneck turns out to be real and broad, it'll come back naturally.
- Don't try to resolve whether staleness or duplication is distorting the bar. Nobody knows yet, and pressing it now will produce more guessing. Flag it as an open question and move on.
