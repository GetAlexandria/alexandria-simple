# For the Director — Raven's marching orders

## What to put in front of him

Hand him `runtime/problem-framing.md` as-is. The draft frames three problems from the onboarding conversation — empty-dashboard confusion, the data-source step as a wall, and the checklist not reaching users. The evidence is decent but not airtight. Your job is to draw out the thin spots.

## What to draw out

### Thin spot 1: "They've already decided the product is complicated" — is that a customer's words or Leo's read?

**Evidence bar:** A specific time a customer said something like "this is complicated" or "I don't know where to start" — before seeing the checklist. Right now Leo is interpreting their behavior; he may be right, but we need the customer's voice, not the CSM's gloss.

**The move:** "Leo said customers decide the product is complicated before they ever see the checklist. Can you tell me about a specific time a customer said that — what did they actually say, and when in the flow were they?"

If he gives a hand-wavey answer ("it happens all the time," "you can just tell"), the follow-up is: "Can you tell me about one specific time that happened recently?"

### Thin spot 2: Is the data-source dropout Leo's segment, or company-wide?

**Evidence bar:** One other CSM or one other data point (support tickets, drop-off analytics at that step) confirming the same pattern. Leo's evidence is strong for his book — four calls, three accounts dark — but it's one person's accounts.

**The move:** "Leo's accounts are going dark at the data-source step — is that what you're seeing across the board, or is it possible that's his segment? Has anyone else flagged the same step?"

If he says "yeah it's everywhere" without a specific instance, the follow-up is: "Do you have the drop-off data by step, or has another CSM mentioned it?"

### What not to push on

The 31% activation number and the checklist placement are solid — Sam pulled the data and confirmed where it lives. Don't spend time re-establishing those. The chain-of-problems guess in the draft is held loosely and labeled as such; let him react to it but don't defend it.
