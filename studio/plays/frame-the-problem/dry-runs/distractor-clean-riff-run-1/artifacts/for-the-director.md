# For the Director — Raven's marching orders

## What to put in front of him

The draft framing at `runtime/problem-framing.md`. The core is solid: two
first-hand instances from Keiko and Dara, same pattern, same cost shape. The
problem is real and clearly stated. Tell him: "Here's where I landed — two
problems, really one cause and its consequence. I want to check two things with
you before I call this done."

## What to draw out

### 1. The breadth claim — is this bigger than one team?

**Evidence bar:** One specific recent instance from a team other than Keiko and
Dara's.

**The move:** Mira mentioned the DevEx guild had this on the agenda. Put it to
him directly:

> "Mira says other teams are hitting this too. Can you — or can Mira — point me
> to a specific time someone on another team lost hours to the same bare-status-code
> problem? It doesn't have to be the exact date — just one real instance."

If he can't produce one, note the breadth claim as unearned and keep the framing
scoped to the one team. That's still a real problem — just a different priority
conversation.

### 2. The solution direction — what's actually in the team's control?

**Evidence bar:** This isn't about a past instance — it's about whether the
implicit solution (Vanta returns richer errors) is something the team can
actually cause to happen.

**The move:**

> "The framing points at Vanta's error responses as the root cause. Before we go
> further — does the team have any lever to change what Vanta sends back? An API
> feedback channel, a planned version, a contact? Or should we be framing a
> solution that's on our side — better client-side logging, a shared debugging
> playbook, something like that?"

This isn't challenging his solution — it's asking whether the problem framing
should orient toward a solution the team owns or one they'd need to advocate for
externally. That changes the shape of the next conversation.
