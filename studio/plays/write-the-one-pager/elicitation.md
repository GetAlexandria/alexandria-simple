### Decision 1 — React to §6 draft language

**Question:** Does the §6 prompt language capture your intent, or does it
need adjustment before it goes to the Author?

**Stakes:** §6 is the single most Director-owned section of any brief — it
holds the tone, the method emphasis, the hard limits in the play's own voice.
The orchestrator drafted from the ratified brief content and the prompt.md
exemplar, but judgment calls about emphasis (what to lead with, where to be
blunt, where to be warm) belong to the Director. The Author can't polish what
was never intended.

**Options:**

A. **Approve as drafted** — the draft correctly reflects the ratified brief;
   proceed to Author/Checker.
   + Fast path to Gate 1
   − The Director hasn't seen the words; approval without reaction is a
     formal gap

★ **B. React and revise (recommended)** — Director reads §6 and flags any
   tone, emphasis, or omission mismatches; orchestrator refines before
   Gate 1.
   + Ensures §6 is truly Director-owned, not just Director-ratified upstream
   + Catches anything the orchestrator compressed or missed
   − One additional back-and-forth before Gate 1

C. **Defer §6 to the Author entirely** — remove the proposed draft, let the
   Author work from the brief alone.
   + Consistent with the Author's job as described in README
   − §6 exists precisely because the Director's first-pass intent matters;
     an empty §6 puts more weight on the Author and the lint cycle

**Recommendation reasoning:** The experiment's purpose is Director reaction
to a built page, not a blank field. Option B delivers that. Option A skips
the reaction; Option C abandons the elicitation-review model for this section.

### Decision 2 — Final artifact name

**Question:** What is the artifact this play produces called — "one-pager,"
"PRD," "product brief," or something else?

**Stakes:** The Director's own step-0 note flags this as a Gate-1-era design
call: "research stays grounded in PRDs and one-pagers, the slang the industry
actually speaks. Final artifact name is a Gate-1-era design call." The
grounding doc (§1) confirms "solution requirements document" has no standing
currency; "one-pager" and "PRD" are the live options. The name bakes into the
prompt, the index.html header, and every downstream reference.

**Options:**

A. **"One-pager"** — the brief form, explicit about scope.
   + Signals scope clearly: this is the single-page form, not the full PRD
   + Matches the v1 form ruling (Director, 2026-06-11): "taking the work
     that's already been done on problem and building up solution parameters"
   − May feel informal in some stakeholder contexts

★ **B. "One-Pager / PRD" (recommended, per current brief title and grounding)**
   — the compound name the play already carries.
   + Preserves the form family without pre-deciding the depth question
   + The grounding doc §1 confirms both names are industry-standard
   + Depth-scaled versions in §8 can grow into the "PRD" half naturally
   − Slightly ambiguous about which form a given run produces

C. **"Product brief"** — neutral, industry-recognised.
   + Works across feature / service / product scope
   + No depth connotation
   − Less distinctive than "one-pager"; may blend with other plays' outputs

**Recommendation reasoning:** The brief and workshop page already use "Write
the One-Pager / PRD"; maintaining that until the Director rules at Gate 1
avoids a premature rename. Option B is the status-quo-until-ruled position.

### Decision 3 — Tier

**Question:** What tier is this play — coordinator, manager, or senior?

**Stakes:** The tier signals complexity, judgment depth, and the expected
effort to harden and prove. It feeds into the registry and the grading rubric.

**Options:**

A. **Coordinator** — straightforward execution, low ambiguity.
   − This play consumes a complex artifact (the problem brief), builds a
     coverage map, handles disputes, and enforces the sizing law. That is

B. **Manager** — moderate judgment, some ambiguity.
   + Matches rung 1's tier (Frame the Problem is manager-tier, proven)

   − The compound nature (six declared-TBD inputs, nine moves, sizing

★ **C. Senior (recommended, orchestrator proposal)** — high judgment,
