# Research brief — Write the One-Pager / PRD (step 0)

Drafted 2026-06-11 by the orchestrator from the Director's step-0 frame
(agreed same day). This is the first research run under the **ground before
design** rule (README, Director ruling 2026-06-11) and the first to carry
its second mandate, ruled by the Director at the same kickoff:

> Research not only the best practices for our output (its form, its
> attributes) but also explores the questions we ask in the elicitation
> we're about to do — filling out our interview manifest with expert
> answers and examples found online.

So this brief has two mandates:

**A. Ground the output.** What the artifact *is* — its forms, structures,
attributes, and when each form fits.

**B. Pre-answer the elicitation.** The brief conversation runs on
`../../TEMPLATE-brief.md`'s sections. For each, find the expert answer
before asking the Director, so design time is spent ruling on researched
options, not generating from scratch:
- §1 Goal — what a successful run of "write the PRD/one-pager" produces;
  the done-condition; what a *failed* one looks like.
- §2 Trigger — when, in expert practice, this step fires in a product
  process (what must exist first; what fires it).
- §3 Required knowledge — the inputs experts say you need in hand, and
  what practitioners do when one is missing.
- §4 Golden path — the expert step-by-step method for writing one.
- §5 What could go wrong — documented failure modes and **root causes of
  failure**, with severity.
- §7 Proof spec — how experts judge one: review checklists, rubrics, what
  separates a strong example from a weak one.

**The naming question (Director-raised):** we may run this play for
features *or services*, so "product requirements document" may be too
narrow — is there an established broader term ("solution requirements
document"? "solution design document"?) or is PRD the slang the industry
actually speaks regardless of scope? Map the landscape; the Director rules
at design time. Research stays grounded in PRDs and similar regardless.

## Segments (one Sonnet researcher each)

1. **Form & the naming landscape** — what a PRD is, what a product
   one-pager is, the adjacent forms (PR-FAQ, SRS, solution requirements
   document, design doc/RFC, lean canvas-style one-pagers), canonical
   section structures, and when each form fits (incl. one-pager vs full
   PRD).
2. **The golden path** — expert step-by-step processes for producing one
   (Amazon Working Backwards / PR-FAQ; Cagan; published PM guides), the
   prerequisite inputs, who writes and who reviews.
3. **Failure modes & root causes** — why these documents fail, named
   anti-patterns, any surveys/evidence, practitioner critiques of the form
   itself.
4. **Judging quality** — review checklists and rubrics; goals/non-goals
   discipline; success-metrics framing; what reviewers actually check.
5. **Worked examples** — real published PRDs/one-pagers and respected
   templates; what structure they share; one dissected good example and,
   if findable, a documented bad one.

## Claim discipline (inherited from rung 1's salvage)

Every claim carries its source (URL + who said it); quotes verbatim;
primary sources preferred and fetched where possible; anything
unverifiable is flagged, not dropped or laundered. Raw claims land in
`extracted-claims.md`; a verification pass checks the load-bearing claims;
confirmed material graduates to `grounding.md`, caveats inline — the
rung-1 pattern (`../../frame-the-problem/research/grounding.md`).
