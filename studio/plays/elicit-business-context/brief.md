## 1. Goal

One run consumes the stated business need, a stakeholder list with decision
rights, and any existing documentation, and produces **one analysis, rendered
twice**:

- **The captured business context record** *(the filed artifact — exhaustive)*:
  a thin, structured document (1–2 pages) that answers six questions — what
  problem the business is trying to solve, why now, what the appetite is (time
  and resource ceiling), what constraints exist across functional domains, what
  assumptions are being made, and who has authority to approve the solution.
  The record feeds directly into Write the One-Pager / PRD as the "business
  context / why-now" input that play's grounding.md §8 marks TBD.
- **The spoken read-back** *(essential; 100 words is a ceiling, not a target)*:
  Raven's in-flow summary for the room — the context gestalt, the appetite,
  and the why-now in plain language people can react to aloud. It is the
  artifact's voice, never a second opinion; it may claim nothing the record
  doesn't contain. Director ruling 2026-06-12: spoken read-back on every
  rung-2 input play; ceiling 100 (raised from rung 1's 75), per-play scaling
  delegated to orchestrator judgment.

The spoken paragraph may never claim anything the record doesn't contain
(one analysis, two renderings — anti-drift rule, adopted from rung 1).

Done when the record passes all ten eyeball checks in §7 of the grounding:
single-sentence problem statement with no solution language, why-now named as
a condition not an aspiration, appetite stated as a fixed number, success
criteria framed as behavioral or metric outcomes, constraints and assumptions
separated and labeled, stakeholder statements attributed as opinions not
facts, strategic alignment named to a specific OKR or priority, decision
authority identified, length at most 1–2 pages, and at least one explicit
open question present. Plus: the spoken paragraph is composed, claims nothing
the record doesn't contain, and is within the 100-word ceiling.

**Failed run:** the play was invoked without a stated business need or a
stakeholder list, or the available material contains only slogans and no
falsifiable claims — the artifact cannot meet checks 1, 2, or 3 without
inventing content. Failure is loud and specific: report what is present,
what is missing, what form the missing material would need to take, then
stop. A degraded artifact that cannot pass checks 1, 2, or 3 would pollute
the one-pager downstream; stopping cleanly is the lesser harm.

**DIRECTOR DECISION — see decision queue:** whether this play is a required
gate before Write the One-Pager or an optional enrichment with degraded-and-labeled
fallback. The grounding offers both positions; research-brief.md question 1
names it open.

## 2. Trigger

The play fires when a new initiative, feature, or bet has been named but
before Write the One-Pager begins. Specifically: the chain requires both a
problem brief from Frame the Problem and a captured business context record
from this play before the one-pager can be written. This play provides the
second input.

It also fires as a stand-alone play when a stakeholder arrives with a
solution already in hand and the business context has not been made explicit —
the reversed-OST condition described in grounding.md §3 Move 6.

**What fires it in the meeting:** name-call ("Raven, get the business
context on this") or a chain hand-off from Frame the Problem. The exact
invocation form for Freeq is unresolved pending the Director's chain-position
ruling.

Grounded: grounding.md §7 (§2 Trigger), §8

## 4. Golden path — the moves

**The story:** Raven starts at the desk, not in the room. She scans what's
already written down so she doesn't use executive time on facts already on
file. Then she draws a map of who holds which constraint — finance, legal,
sales, marketing, BD, brand, leadership — and lines up the conversations
before any group session happens. In those conversations she listens for
constraints, not solutions: she explains that the better she understands
the limits, the better the eventual solution will fit. She sets the appetite
first, before scope enters the room — because once scope is on the table,
any budget conversation becomes a cutting fight. When a stakeholder arrives
with a solution already in hand, she doesn't accept the frame; she reverses
it, asking what problem the solution solves and where that insight came from.
After each conversation she logs open questions immediately and shares them
back. Then she synthesizes the record: the six fields, the ten checks, the
open items explicit at the bottom.

```
1. review_docs — judgment — reads existing strategy docs, roadmaps,
     contracts, compliance obligations (supplied set) — identifies what is
     already known vs. what requires stakeholder input — writes a
     pre-brief gap list.
     MISSING docs: log what is absent; proceed to Move 2 with explicit
     gaps noted.

2. map_stakeholders — judgment — reads stakeholder list with roles and
     decision rights — assigns each domain (finance / legal / sales /
     marketing / BD / brand / leadership) to a named stakeholder; flags
     any domain with no named holder — writes a domain-to-stakeholder map.
     [Doer: judgment because determining which person holds which
     functional constraint requires comprehension of the role descriptions,
     not a closed rule.]

3. interview_1on1 — judgment — reads domain-to-stakeholder map + pre-brief
     gap list — conducts one-on-one constraint interviews per stakeholder
     (not group sessions; NNGroup failure mode); topic areas: success
     metrics, priorities, history/expertise/constraints, process
     preferences — NOT requirements directly (grounding.md §3 Move 3) —
     writes raw interview notes per stakeholder, each answer attributed
     and marked as opinion/hypothesis, never as fact.
     GROUP SESSION attempted: decline; note the NNGroup failure mode;
     reschedule one-on-one.

4. set_appetite — judgment — reads raw interview notes — asks the appetite
     question before any scope discussion: "How much time is this worth to
     us, strategically?" (Shape Up pattern: appetites start with a number,
     end with a design; grounding.md §3 Move 4) — writes the declared
     appetite (fixed time/resource ceiling or explicit "appetite not yet
     declared — see open questions").
     DECISION CLASSIFICATION: appetite stated as scope-derived → mechanical
     flag; kick back to Move 4 to reframe; log as taste call, surface at
     gate.

5. surface_whynow — judgment — reads raw interview notes + pre-brief gap
     list — runs the why-now and assumption surface (Amazon internal FAQ
     frame: what assumptions must be true; top three reasons this won't
     succeed; upfront investment and risk management; what challenging
     problems — business model, legal, engineering — need to be solved;
     Cagan Opportunity Assessment: why-now market window, grounding.md §3
     Move 5) — writes a why-now field + assumption list, each assumption
     labeled as such.

6. handle_reversed_ost — judgment — reads raw interview notes — detects
     whether any stakeholder arrived solution-first (a solution framed as
     context, which is Root cause 4 in grounding.md §4) — applies
     reversed-OST (Lab Zero / MindTheProduct): "What customer is this for?
     What problem does it solve? Where did that insight come from? What
     must be true for this to succeed? Which assumption feels the most
     uncertain?" (grounding.md §3 Move 6, CONFIRMED-PRIMARY) — writes
     surfaced-context additions to raw notes.
     [Orchestrator call — ratification owed: whether this is an embedded
     conditional move or a sub-play is an open Director question. Embedded
     here pending ruling; see decision queue.]

7. record_authority — judgment — reads raw interview notes — captures
     budget owner (HolaBrief: "Where is this budget coming from?" and "Is
     the budget owner involved?"), decision authority, and stakeholder veto
     rights (DACI framework pattern; grounding.md §3 Move 7) — writes
     authority field.

8. confirm_openq — judgment — reads raw interview notes + all fields so
     far — runs the Torres close per stakeholder: "Has anything changed
     since we last agreed on this outcome?" and "Did I miss anything?"
     (grounding.md §3 Move 8) — writes open-questions section (the record
     treats itself as a starting point, not a closed spec).

9. synthesize — judgment — reads all moves' outputs — composes the
     captured business context record: six fields (problem diagnosis /
     why-now with named trigger / declared appetite / domain constraints /
     explicit assumptions / decision authority), passes the ten-check
     rubric (grounding.md §5) inline as a self-check, emits open-questions
     section explicitly — writes the record file.

10. ground_check — software — reads synthesized record + raw notes —
     closed rules: every stakeholder statement attributed, not presented as
     fact; appetite stated as a number, not derived from scope; problem
     statement contains no solution language; constraints section present;
     open-questions section present; length at most 2 pages — writes
     annotated record (per-field check status).
     Failures: bounce to owning move once; if it still fails, emit marked
     failing — degraded and labeled, never silently dropped.

11. render     — judgment — reads annotated record
               — composes the spoken paragraph: opens by naming the context
                 gestalt and why-now in plain language; carries the appetite
                 as a concrete number (or the honest absence); names what is
                 still open; claims nothing the record doesn't back; closes
                 with one question aimed at the record's weakest point; 100
                 words is a ceiling, not a target (Director ruling 2026-06-12)
               — writes the paragraph

12. pause      — judgment — re-reads the paragraph against the annotated record
               — the check before speaking: does the paragraph claim anything
                 the record doesn't back? does it state more certainty than the
                 attribution grades support? does it take a side on anything the
                 record leaves open? does it exceed the 100-word ceiling — and
                 if so, which thought goes, whole? does it end with exactly one
                 question? correct before speaking, then speak
               — writes pass, or corrects and re-reads once
```

**Doer honesty note:** Moves 1–9 are all judgment because they require
comprehension of domain-specific material, evaluation of stakeholder
candor, and selection among options with no closed rule. Move 10 is
mechanical: it applies a finite, defined checklist that a machine could
follow. Per the prototype rule of thumb (README): Move 10 is currently
covered best-effort by an agent and is pegged **future software**.

Grounded: grounding.md §3 (eight moves), §2, §4 (root causes and counter-practices), §7 (§4)

## 5. What could go wrong

Two playbook-wide defaults apply unless a row overrides them (README,
field-review rules): a loop that fails to fix the same defect three times
freezes and kicks to the Director with what was tried; every decision an
agent meets is classified — *mechanical* (decide silently, log),
*taste* (decide, surface at next gate), *Director-challenge* (never
auto-decided).

| Hypothesis | Severity | Response |
|---|---|---|
| No stated business need — play invoked too early | errored | Loud, specific failure; stop; surface the gap; report what is missing and what form it would need to take |
| Stakeholder list absent | needs-input | Ask the Director before proceeding; do not degrade silently |
| Stakeholder input is solution-framed context (Root cause 4 — HiPPO mechanism) | low-confidence | Move 6 (handle_reversed_ost) activates; log detection; surface at gate |
| Appetite deferred: "scope isn't scoped yet" — Root cause 3 | errored | Refuse the scope-first framing (must be refused, not degraded; grounding.md §7 §5); attempt re-framing once; if stakeholder holds firm, log as open question, kick to Director |
| Strategy delivered as slogan, no diagnosis — Root cause 2 (Rumelt) | low-confidence | Ground check flags check 2 and check 7 failures; bounce to synthesize; if still fails, emit marked failing |
| Stakeholder statement laundered into fact — Root cause 1 | errored | Ground check catches attribution failure; bounce to synthesize once |
| Group session attempted instead of 1:1 interviews | low-confidence | Log NNGroup failure mode; decline the group format; note the degraded-candor risk in the open-questions section |
| Why-now absent (no market window, no named trigger) | low-confidence | Ground check flags check 2 failure; bounce once; if still empty, emit marked failing (missing why-now will degrade the one-pager — flag explicitly) |
| Constraints section omitted (common omission per ParallelHQ) | errored | Ground check flags; bounce to synthesize once |
| Record exceeds 2 pages (crosses into PRD territory — Productboard) | low-confidence | Ground check flags check 9 failure; synthesize must trim or consolidate; taste call, surface at gate |
| Context treated as closed spec (Root cause 5 — one-and-done capture) | low-confidence | The open-questions field structurally prevents this; if the section is empty, ground check bounces once |
| Stakeholder retroactively changes a constraint after the session | needs-input | Torres close (Move 8) surfaces this in-session; post-session: treat as a new input, re-run affected moves, log the change |
| Three-strikes freeze | timed-out | Preserve state; kick to Director with what was tried and what the defect is |
| Spoken paragraph overclaims or inflates certainty on vivid stakeholder quotes (commitment-inflation analog) | low-confidence | `pause` move corrects once before speaking; grader checklist catches the rest. Known residual from rung-1 pattern — adopted from play 1's grader checklist. |

**Decision classification reminder:** appetite-stated-as-scope-derived is
a *mechanical* flag (Move 4 handles it silently and logs). Reversed-OST
detection is *taste* (surface at gate). Any stakeholder constraint that
would change the play's design is *Director-challenge* (never auto-decided).

Grounded: grounding.md §4 (root causes 1–5 and counter-practices), §3 (Block vs. proceed rule), §7 (§5)

## 6. Draft prompt language

Proposed for reaction — this section is Director-owned; these words are a
starting point, not a ruling.

**Core framing (grounded draft, for Author reaction):**

> Your job in this conversation is to understand what the business is
> betting on and why, before any design work begins. Stakeholder input is
> a hypothesis, not a specification. The executive may know what they want
> built, but your job is the constraint behind the want — the time they're
> willing to spend, the market window they're pointing at, the risks they
> think will sink it.
>
> Start before the meeting, not in it. Scan whatever documentation exists.
> Then, in one-on-one conversations — not group sessions — explain that the
> better you understand their constraints, the better the eventual solution
> will fit. Ask "How much time is this worth, strategically?" before any
> scope discussion begins. Once scope is on the table, any budget
> conversation becomes a negotiation over cuts. Appetites start with a
> number and end with a design; scope starts with a wish and ends with
> a fight.
>
> When a stakeholder arrives with a solution already in mind — and they
> often will — do not accept the frame. Ask what customer the solution is
> for, what problem it solves, where that insight came from, and which
> assumption feels most uncertain. You are not here to build what you were
> handed; you are here to understand what problem it was an answer to.
>
> The artifact you produce names what has changed in the world, why the
> current state is inadequate, what the business is willing to spend, what
> domain constraints exist (finance, legal, sales, marketing, BD, brand),
> what assumptions are being made (labeled as such), who has authority to
> approve the solution, and what is still open. Thin is right. One or two
> pages. If it's longer, it has crossed into PRD territory and lost its
> alignment function.

**Protected raw material from grounding (verbatim; Author may rephrase
within grounding):**
- "Stakeholder input is a hypothesis, not a specification." (synthesized
  from BABOK, Cagan, Torres, Brown — grounding.md §2)
- "Explain that the better you understand their constraints, the better
  our solutions will be." (Cagan, grounding.md §3 Move 3)
- "Has anything changed since we last agreed on this outcome?" (Torres,

- "What are the top three reasons this will not succeed?" (Amazon internal

- Reversed-OST questions: "What customer is this for? What problem does

**Posture:** the grounding describes the PM's role as active
constraint-surfacing, not transcription. Manager tier per this brief: owns
the call within the elicitation scope; analyst's rigor plus a stated
synthesis. Skepticism is a deliberate interviewer stance (Dan Brown /
EightShapes: "uncovering assumptions by validating organizational norms
rather than accepting them as self-evident," grounding.md §2).

**PROPOSED — render / pause moves (Director-owned; proposed language for
Author reaction; Director ruling 2026-06-12 sets the shape):**

> **Render (step 11).** The record is filed. Now say what matters to the
> room — not a recap of their own words, but what the analysis added: the
> picture that emerged from the constraints, the appetite as a real number
> (or the honest absence of one), the why-now in a phrase they can hold.
> If an assumption is the riskiest thing in the room, name it as such and
> point at the record. Close with one question aimed at the place the
> record is weakest — the constraint domain with no named holder, the
> assumption that has never been tested, the appetite that came out as a
> range instead of a number. 100 words is a ceiling, not a target. A
> simple context earns a short paragraph; when it runs long, cut a thought
> entire, never compress one.

> **Pause (step 12 — before speaking).** Re-read the paragraph against the
> record. Does it claim anything the record doesn't back? Does it state
> more certainty than the attribution ("stated as opinion," "hypothesis")
> warrants? Does it take a side on anything the record leaves open — a
> constraint still disputed, an assumption still untested? Does it exceed
> 100 words — and if so, which whole thought goes? Does it end with
> exactly one question? Correct once; then speak.

## 7. Proof spec

**Fixture:** DIRECTOR DECISION — see decision queue. The grounding's §7
describes the fixture shape: "a stakeholder brief that passes checks 1–3
and 5–8 but fails checks 4, 9, and 10 (delivery milestone instead of
outcome, too long, no open questions). Correct behavior: flag the three
failures, do not invent what the outcomes or open questions should be."
A fixture matching that description does not yet exist in `fixtures/`.

**Pass looks like:** the artifact passes all ten eyeball checks in
grounding.md §5. For the Director to eyeball:

1. The problem statement names who and what, with no solution language
   (Check 1).
2. Why-now is answered with a named condition — a specific market event,
   loss, or window — not a generic aspiration (Check 2).
3. Appetite is a fixed number or range stated before scope was discussed
   (Check 3).
4. Success criteria are behavioral or metric outcomes, not delivery
   milestones (Check 4).
5. Constraints and assumptions appear in separate labeled sections (Check 5).
6. Every stakeholder statement is attributed as an opinion or hypothesis,
   never as a confirmed fact (Check 6).
7. Strategic alignment names a specific OKR or priority, not a slogan
   (Check 7).
8. Decision authority identifies who approves, who can veto, whether the
   budget owner is present (Check 8).
9. The artifact is 1–2 pages (Check 9).
10. At least one explicit open question is present (Check 10).

**Spoken read-back eyeball checks** *(adopted from rung 1's proven pattern;
Director ruling 2026-06-12):*

11. The spoken paragraph is within the 100-word ceiling (closed check).
12. The paragraph claims nothing the record doesn't back (anti-drift rule;
    any statement traceable to a stakeholder opinion or hypothesis must be
    attributed or hedged — not stated as fact).
13. The paragraph takes no side on anything the record leaves open — no
    assertion on a constraint still disputed, no resolution of an assumption
    still labeled "untested."
14. The paragraph ends with exactly one question, aimed at the record's
    weakest point.

**The failure we'll demonstrate:** invoke the play on a stakeholder brief
that contains only Rumelt-class slogans ("aligns with our growth strategy
and customer-centricity pillar") with no diagnosis, no named why-now
trigger, and no appetite. Correct behavior: flag specifically which checks
fail (at minimum checks 1, 2, 3, 7), report what form each missing item
would need to take, do not invent the missing content, stop.

**DIRECTOR DECISION — see decision queue:** fixture authorship is blocked
pending the chain-position ruling (required gate vs. optional enrichment)
and the artifact-form ruling (live guided conversation vs. post-interview
synthesis). Those decisions change what the fixture looks like.

  rule of thumb (README): currently covered best-effort by an agent. The
  ten-check rubric is genuinely mechanical; a future software node would
  run it deterministically. Earning condition: when the play graduates to
  a Fabro graph.

- **Spoken rendering — RESOLVED 2026-06-12.** Whether this play carries
  a spoken read-back was open in the orchestrator-prefilled draft. Director
  ruling 2026-06-12: every rung-2 input play carries a spoken read-back
  alongside its filed artifact, two-renderings shape proven on play 1
  (frame-the-problem). Ceiling 100 words for this play (raised from rung 1's

  added to §4; spoken eyeball checks 11–14 added to §7; failure row added

  nearest analog; whether this play warrants an explicit delta layer is
