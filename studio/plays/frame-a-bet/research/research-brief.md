**How this play was surfaced.** "Frame a Bet" was already in the inventory
(strategy tier, senior audience). It emerged as a compound input from two
directions: (1) Write the One-Pager's goals-and-metrics section cannot be
filled with conviction until the team has a named hypothesis and a
pre-registered success metric — Frame a Bet is the upstream play that
produces those inputs; (2) Scope an MVP (rung 3) repeatedly blocks because
there is no named hypothesis governing what the MVP is designed to test.
Additionally, the play was predicted to drive roughly half of the Calibration
Ledger — the artifact where bet outcomes are tracked against stated hypotheses
to measure team forecast accuracy over time.

**Mandate A — Ground the output.** What the bet artifact *is*: its canonical
forms, required elements, falsifiability criteria, and the decision it must
enable. What a failed bet document looks like.

**Mandate B — Pre-answer the elicitation.** The brief conversation runs on
`../../TEMPLATE-brief.md`'s sections. For each, find the expert answer before
asking the Director, so design time is spent ruling on researched options:
- §1 Goal — what a successful run of "frame a bet" produces; what done looks
  like; what a failed one looks like.
- §2 Trigger — when, in expert practice, this play fires (what must exist
  first; what fires it; what it blocks if absent).
- §3 Required knowledge — inputs experts say you need in hand; what to do
  when one is missing.
- §4 Golden path — the expert step-by-step method for producing a bet
  document.
- §5 What could go wrong — documented failure modes and root causes, with
  severity.
- §7 Proof spec — how experts judge a bet: rubrics, the falsifiability test,
  what separates a strong example from a weak one.
