# Research brief — Scope an MVP (step 0)

Drafted 2026-06-11 by the orchestrator. Frame below is orchestrator-stated
under the Director's standing instruction of the same day ("keep laying
down ground — run this process on the other two must-have rungs");
**Director ratification of the frame is owed at review** before the brief
conversation proceeds. Two mandates, per the rung-2 pattern: ground the
output AND pre-answer the elicitation. Plus this run's third mandate,
Director-added: **surface compound plays in disguise** — distinct
activities inside the method that have their own artifact and trigger.

**The frame (to ratify):** rung 3 consumes the one-pager (rung 2's
definition) and cuts to the smallest slice that delivers the outcome and
earns the learning — emitting a scope cut: what v1 is, what is explicitly
deferred and why, and what this slice proves. Success looks like:
engineering could start; every cut is traceable and deliberate; the
learning goal is explicit. A failed run looks like: MVP bloat, horizontal
slices that demo nothing, or silent cuts nobody agreed to.

## Segments (Sonnet researchers)

1. **Form & method canon** — what an MVP actually is (Ries: validated
   learning, not a small version), Shape Up appetite and scope hammering,
   story mapping and the walking skeleton (Patton), SLC (Cohen) as the
   counter-school, MoSCoW/vertical slicing; what the emitted scoping
   artifact looks like in expert practice.
2. **The golden path** — the expert step-by-step from definition doc to
   scope cut: outcome anchoring, riskiest-assumption tests, slicing
   heuristics, who participates (PM + eng + design), required inputs,
   missing-input conventions.
3. **Failure modes & root causes** — MVP bloat, "MVP = phase 1 of
   everything," cargo-cult MVPs with no learning goal, cutting quality
   instead of scope, RAT-vs-MVP confusion, the SLC critique of bad MVPs.
4. **Judging quality + worked examples** — rubrics a non-developer can
   eyeball on a scope cut; published story maps / Shape Up pitches /
   release plans; weak-vs-strong contrasts for the future example gallery.

All segments: claim discipline inherited from rung 2 (CLAIM / QUOTE
verbatim / SOURCE / CONFIDENCE; primaries fetched; unverified flagged) and
the compound-candidates mandate. Verification pass on load-bearing
snippet-only claims before synthesis. Raw claims → `extracted-claims.md`;
cited canon + pre-answered elicitation manifest → `grounding.md`.

## Decision briefs for the Director (surfaced by this grounding)

**1. Should "Design the Riskiest-Assumption Test" become an inventory
slot?** Stakes: the canon (Higham's RAT, GDS risk-scoring, Ries's smoke
tests) treats experiment design as a discrete deliverable — test card:
hypothesis, cheapest experiment, success/failure metric, decision rule —
and nothing in the inventory owns it; without a home, rung 3's "what does
v1 prove?" gate has no upstream play when the hypothesis is weak.
Options: (a) add the slot (insight, manager) — RECOMMENDED: it is the
same shape as Elicit Business Context (a real, missing, compounded
input), and its grounding is already being researched; con: inventory
growth before any version of it has run. (b) Treat it as a move inside
Scope an MVP — pro: no new surface; con: the artifact (test card) and
trigger (assumption identified, pre-build) are clearly distinct from the
scope cut. (c) Park it — pro: zero cost now; con: the gap re-surfaces at
every rung-3 run with a weak hypothesis.

**2. Where does the scope-increase review live?** Stakes: the canon's
change-control gate (the six-question checklist before any addition to a
locked scope) prevents the documented scope-creep-back failure; it has no
inventory home. Options: (a) a standing gate written into rung 3's brief
(§5 response + §8 note) — RECOMMENDED: it is a rule about a locked
artifact, not a recurring job; con: other plays with locked artifacts
will each need their own copy until it's promoted. (b) A new inventory
slot (ops or strategy) — pro: one home; con: it would be a play that
exists only to say no, with no demo moment. (c) Fold into Keep the
Roadmap Current — pro: adjacent; con: muddies that play's job.
