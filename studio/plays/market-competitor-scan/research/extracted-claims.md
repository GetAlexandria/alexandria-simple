## Method report claims

### Cadence and scope

- Competitive intelligence is an ongoing function, not a one-off effort. [F]
  kompyte.com/blog/how-much-time-competitive-intelligence
- Most common CI cadence among PMMs surveyed: quarterly 39.8%, monthly 23.7%,
  weekly 21.5%, annually 10.8%. [F] kompyte.com (same source)
- Recommended cadence map: weekly 15-minute flag; monthly win/loss pattern
  review; quarterly feature gap matrix update feeding roadmap; annually
  strategic positioning. [F] userintuition.ai/reference-guides/competitive-
  intelligence-for-product-managers
- The trigger for a scan determines its required depth; a full teardown fits
  rethinking the roadmap; positioning refinement needs only pricing, messaging,
  and tone. "Start by identifying the trigger for your analysis." [F]
  userintuition.ai (same source)
- Treating CI as a one-time project is the critical mistake; a six-month-old
  analysis may no longer reflect the market. "The critical mistake many product
  managers make is treating CI as a one-time project." [F] userintuition.ai
- A one-time analysis becomes outdated within months and loses decision
  relevance. [F] plane.so/blog/competitive-analysis-for-product-managers-
  a-complete-guide

### Scoping the competitive set

- JTBD framework redefines the competitive set as any solution the customer
  "hires" to do the same job, including non-consumption. [F] thrv.com/blog/
  clayton-christensen-jobs-to-be-done
- Non-consumption — choosing not to act — is "the most important competitor
  of all" per Christensen Institute. "The way we define competition...leaves
  out the most important competitor of all: nonconsumption." [F]
  christenseninstitute.org/blog/non-consumption-is-your-fiercest-competition
- JTBD asks whether customers are "cobbling together" alternatives, signaling
  fragmented landscape ripe for consolidation. "Are customers cobbling together
  solutions to get the entire job done?" [F] strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done
- Jobs are more stable scan targets than product categories: "While products
  come and go, the underlying job does not go away." [F] strategyn.com
- In SaaS, competing against non-consumption means targeting users who
  currently have no software-enabled capability; requires asking where new
  budget comes from. [F] tomtunguz.com/nonconsumption
- Competitor tiering into direct / indirect / aspirational (confirmed-primary;
  see verification verdicts). [F] aakashg.com

### Data gathering — primary sources

- Competitor changelogs and release notes are "the most reliable primary
  source for product intelligence available publicly" — they reveal actual
  engineering investment decisions. [F] trackmore.io/blog/what-is-competitor-
  changelog-why-should-you-care
- "Most features don't appear randomly. They're built because customers asked
  for them, because deals were lost without them, or because churn analysis
  revealed them as a gap." [F] trackmore.io (same source)
- Competitor pricing pages should be monitored daily (43% change monthly per
  one estimate). "A pricing restructure tells you more about a competitor's
  strategy in one page than a month of blog posts." [F] visualping.io/blog/
  competitive-intelligence-sources
- API documentation pages are "the most honest pages" competitors publish,
  revealing new endpoints, deprecated features, rate limit changes, version
  bumps before marketing announcements. [F] visualping.io
- Job posting volume and department distribution reveal roadmap direction
  before product announcements. "Executive changes are leading indicators. A
  new Chief Revenue Officer means the sales motion is about to change." [F]
  visualping.io
- Primary sources come directly from the competitor's own digital presence;
  third-party sources are external platforms where competitor data surfaces.
  [F] visualping.io

### Data gathering — secondary sources

- G2 and Capterra are secondary sources (third-party platforms). [F] visualping.io
- The practice of using G2's "What do you dislike?" field for competitor
  weaknesses and Capterra for SMB/mid-market personas is corroborated by
  Visualping and Klue [F], though the specific wording from hiresteve.ai is
  UNCONFIRMED (see verification verdicts).
- 82% of software PMs use competitive intelligence in planning (US survey,
  n=100; treat as directional). [F] aqute.com — confirmed-primary.

### Feature gap analysis

- Feature gap analysis is triggered by three situations: roadmap prioritization,
  positioning/messaging refinement, standardizing competitive research. [F]
  productledalliance.com/competitor-feature-gap-analysis-template-framework
- Feature gap analysis has six sequential steps: competitor overview, feature
  comparison matrix, gap identification, customer impact assessment, strategic
  recommendations, implementation planning with owners and timelines. [F]
  productledalliance.com
- Feature comparison matrix should use graduated categories (fully supported /
  partially supported / not available) rather than binary yes/no — CONFIRMED-
  PRIMARY verbatim (see verification verdicts). [F] productboard.com
- Feature gaps must be assessed for customer impact before prioritization.
  Gaps are classified as: primary loss driver (critical priority) / secondary
  factor (secondary priority) / mentioned only (noise, no action required). [F]
  userintuition.ai
- Three detection methods for feature gaps: customer-focused (ask non-
  converting leads), product-focused (compare engagement to forecasts),
  competitive analysis (assess rivals via market research). [F] productfolio.com
- Feature gap analysis must move beyond marketing claims to identify native
  functionality vs. third-party integrations vs. paid add-ons. [F] aqute.com
- Three gap types to distinguish: misguided flagship feature (features users
  don't value), persona mismatch (wrong segment), buzzword trap (trending
  capabilities that don't meet real expectations). [F] productfolio.com

### Win/loss analysis

- Win/loss analysis: systematic capture and analysis of why deals are won and
  lost; provides primary ground truth for feature gap prioritization. [F]
  pragmaticinstitute.com/product/framework/win-loss-analysis
- Interviews should be conducted within 90 days of the decision event, "when
  insights are still fresh." [F] uservoice.com/blog/winloss-analysis-for-
  product-managers-part-1
- "Only 40% of win-loss interviews end up with a truthful reason." PMs (not
  salespeople) should conduct interviews because buyers are more candid with
  a neutral party. [F] uservoice.com
- Sample must be balanced — equal won and lost deals — to prevent skewed
  findings. [F] uservoice.com/Pragmatic Institute
- Three-phase process: pre-interview (brief with sales), during interview
  (confirm pain points, identify competing firms, selection criteria and
  decision rationale), post-interview (document, distribute, debrief, assign
  actions). [F] pragmaticinstitute.com/resources/articles/product/win-loss-
  analysis-checklist-for-product-managers
- Win/loss findings should be tagged with supporting buyer quotes associated
  to themes, then distributed in real-time to product, sales, and marketing
  teams. "Tag both positive and negative themes from each conversation." [F]
  productmarketingalliance.com/8-tips-for-effective-win-loss-analysis

### JTBD competitive analysis framing

- JTBD competitive analysis reframes the question from "what features does
  the competitor have?" to "how much better or worse is a product at helping
  the customer get a job done?" [F] strategyn.com/outcome-driven-innovation/
  competitive-analysis
- Customers evaluate 50–150 desired-outcome metrics when assessing a product;
  traditional feature analysis misses most. [F] strategyn.com
- "Companies are not competing against other companies or their products.
  They are competing for the customers." [F] strategyn.com
- When a competitor launches a feature, JTBD analysis asks three questions:
  same customer segment? same underlying job? same growth lever? [F]
  thrv.com/blog/the-real-value-of-competitive-analysis

### CI cycle and dissemination

- Five-step CI cycle: orientation, data gathering, analysis and processing,
  reporting, actioning — then repeats. [F] competitiveintelligencealliance.io
- Leonard M. Fuld: "You must gather information constantly, day-in, day-out,
  and not just during the traditional strategic planning cycle…your competitors
  are not so polite as to wait till next year at the same time to once again
  compete." — CONFIRMED-PRIMARY verbatim (see verification verdicts). [F]
  competitiveintelligencealliance.io (citing Fuld, *The New Competitor
  Intelligence*)
- Stakeholder output formats (confirmed-primary from aakashg.com):
  C-Suite/Executives: 1-page executive summary with clear, bold
  recommendations. Engineering Leads: technical deep-dive session, internal
  wiki doc. Marketing Teams: short presentation with key battlecards.
  Sales Teams: live training session, one-sheet cheat sheets. [F] aakashg.com
  NOTE: the original report's framing ("positioning angles" for marketing,
  "battlecards for objection handling" for sales) is a reasonable gloss but
  the primary source wording above is the confirmed form.

## Quality/failure report claims

### Feature parity trap

- The feature parity trap: "You see a competitor launch something, assume the
  market validated it already, and ship a version of the same feature without
  confirming whether your users need it." [F] userpilot.com/blog/feature-
  parity-trap
- "The parity trap occurs when you're so focused on matching features across
  the board that you forget to innovate." [F] nulab.com/learn/design-and-ux/
  feature-parity
- Feature parity as strategy stifles innovation, leads to feature creep,
  ignores user needs, replicates competitors' mistakes, and misallocates
  resources. [F] nulab.com
- Copying features without understanding their value "can lead to spending
  your precious startup resources on something that may not even work." [F]
  medium.com/@shivzb/product-building-traps-part-2-competitive-parity-as-a-
  product-strategy-f3c2f7bc0229
- "If you force your startup to follow a roadmap where less than a majority of
  your bandwidth is spent on innovation, be prepared to not actually be an
  innovator." [F] medium.com/@shivzb (same source)
- "For product managers, it's not about copying competitors. It's about
  understanding market dynamics, spotting gaps, and making strategic decisions
  about where to compete and where to differentiate." [F] productboard.com/
  glossary/competitor-analysis
- "Copying every competitor feature leads to bloated products without clear
  differentiation. Focus on features that matter to your target customers." [F]
  productboard.com
- "True differentiation shows up in customer behavior: why they choose you,
  stay with you, or pay more for you." [F] productboard.com
- "Competitor analysis should inform decisions, not delay them." [F]
  productboard.com

### Scan quality standards

- Feature comparisons alone rarely explain why users choose one product over
  another; quality analysis also evaluates workflows, onboarding, messaging,
  and sentiment. [F] plane.so
- "Competitive analysis loses impact when findings remain descriptive." [F]
  plane.so
- "Depth matters more than quantity. Studying a smaller set thoroughly yields
  deeper insights than scanning a long list superficially." [F] plane.so

### Failure modes

- "Competitive intelligence alone has no value if you don't act on it." [F]
  kompyte.com/blog/top-ci-pitfalls-to-avoid
- Without timely insights, stakeholders are caught off-guard, leading to "lost
  deals or missed product opportunities." [F] kompyte.com
- Without KPIs for CI, organizations cannot determine whether they are meeting
  business objectives. [F] kompyte.com
- Enterprises lose $2–$10 million annually in competitive deals they could
  have won because sales teams lacked the right insights. [F] veridion.com/
  blog-posts/gathering-competitive-intelligence-mistakes (citing unnamed study;
  treat as directional)
- Ignoring indirect competitors and disruptors creates "catastrophic blind
  spots" — Blockbuster/Netflix example. [F] veridion.com
- Collecting data without converting to insights prevents timely decisions —
  Nokia example. [F] veridion.com
- Without clearly defined goals, CI teams "collect everything on all
  competitors without any criteria for filtering and analysis." [F] contify.com/
  resources/blog/3-common-mci-problems-and-their-solutions
- Major competitor updates are "frequently missed or reported too late to take
  action." [F] contify.com
- Stakeholders receive intelligence "either not appropriately packaged for
  their context or completely irrelevant to their role or function." [F]
  contify.com
- A scan without executive sponsorship prevents intelligence from "reaching
  decision-makers effectively." [F] contify.com
- An outdated FDA announcement was republished 14 years after original
  publication and treated as current CI — illustrating how manual verification
  fails. [F] contify.com (case example; no verbatim quote on this specific
  framing)
- 45% of CI analysts surveyed said their input "did not make enough of a
  difference to improve management decision-making." [F] midior.com/is-your-
  competitive-intelligence-actionable
- Intelligence must pass a "so what?" test: "You will lose the battle in the
  final mile if your competitive findings don't pass the 'so what?' test." [F]
  midior.com
- Only half of companies actually utilize the competitive intelligence they
  collect (citing HBR). [F] productschool.com/blog/skills/product-manager-
  competitive-analysis
- Nearly half of sales reps don't know who they're competing with until the
  negotiation stage. [F] klue.com/blog/competitive-intelligence-framework-
  problems
- Decentralized CI information "scattered across 'Google docs,' Slack messages,
  emails, and Zoom calls rather than centralized in a single source of truth."
  [F] klue.com

### Confirmation bias

- Analysts "unconsciously cherry-pick data that supports their preconceived
  notions about competitors or market trends." [F] uncovered.so/blog/
  confirmation-bias-in-competitive-intelligence
- Stakeholder pressure drives analysts to "confirm existing beliefs rather
  than challenge them." [F] uncovered.so
- Confirmation bias creates "a distorted view of the competitive landscape,
  causing analysts to overestimate their own company's strengths." [F]
  uncovered.so
- "Consistently providing biased analyses that fail to accurately predict
  market trends" damages "the credibility of the entire CI function." [F]
  uncovered.so
- Counter-practices: diverse teams, blind analysis, devil's advocate role,
  multiple interpretations. "Always develop multiple interpretations of data
  and explore various potential outcomes." [F] uncovered.so
- Peer-reviewed ISIC 2018 study: analysts form their own hypotheses during
  information needs interviews, rooting biased collection. [F]
  informationr.net/ir/23-4/isic2018/isic1813.html
- "We never go back and clarify what we initially heard" (practitioner Abel,
  same study). [F] informationr.net
- "People think competitive intelligence simply refers to information
  communication technology" (practitioner Steven, same study). [F]
  informationr.net

### JTBD framing of competitive failure

- Traditional feature comparison "is conducted without knowing how customers
  measure value." [F] strategyn.com/outcome-driven-innovation/competitive-
  analysis
- "Feature-to-feature comparisons…merely leads to feature wars, even when the
  features are irrelevant." [F] strategyn.com
- Focusing on a competitor's feature list would have caused analysts to "miss
  the opportunity to eliminate the need for a music store and playlist creation
  altogether" (streaming/Spotify example). [F] thrv.com/blog/the-real-value-
  of-competitive-analysis

### Strategic framing

- Roger Martin: "Strategy is an integrated set of choices that compels a
  desired customer action." [F] lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-ultimate-guide-to-
  strategy-roger-martin
- Without cost leadership or differentiation, competitors can "bully" a
  company; parity confers no defensible position. Diagnostic: "Could customers
  'essentially flip a coin' between you and competitors?" [F] lennysnewsletter
  .com (Roger Martin)
- "Product differentiation is the set of choices that make a product worth
  choosing over alternatives for a specific set of target customers." [F]
  userpilot.com (citing Teresa Torres)
- Competitor marketing claims are unreliable at face value; quality CI "goes
  beyond competitors' marketing claims to extract the truth" about actual
  product capabilities. [F] aqute.com
- Overfixation on external competitor factors rather than internal weaknesses
  or unique strengths is a named strategic risk. [F] simon-kucher.com/en/
  insights/advanced-strategies-effective-competitive-analysis
- "Relying solely on competitive analysis may result in strategic blind spots.
  You may fail to anticipate disruptive forces or emerging trends." [F]
  simon-kucher.com
