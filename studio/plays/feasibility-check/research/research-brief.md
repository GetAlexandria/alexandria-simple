# Research brief — Feasibility Check (rung 2b, stretch)

Drafted 2026-06-11 by the orchestrator; research executed and verified same day.
This play sits at **rung 2b** (stretch) of the golden path — already beyond the
core rung-2 pair — and doubles as the **feasibility side of why-now** for
Write the One-Pager / PRD (rung 2). Its findings feed two downstream consumers:
the Feasibility Check play itself, and the why-now section of rung 2's one-pager.

The brief carries the same two-mandate structure established for Write the
One-Pager (Director ruling 2026-06-11):

> Research not only the best practices for our output (its form, its attributes)
> but also explore the questions we ask in the elicitation we're about to do —
> filling out our interview manifest with expert answers and examples found online.

## Two mandates

**A. Ground the output.** What the feasibility-check artifact *is* — its forms,
structures, the three-value verdict (can / cannot / can-with-cost), what evidence
it must contain, and when it is and is not the right tool.

**B. Pre-answer the elicitation.** The brief conversation runs on
`../../TEMPLATE-brief.md`'s sections. For each, find the expert answer before
asking the Director:
- §1 Goal — what a successful Feasibility Check produces; the done-condition;
  what failure looks like.
- §2 Trigger — when this fires in expert practice; what must exist first; what
  it is NOT triggered by (wrong-tool cases).
- §3 Required knowledge — inputs experts say you need; what to do when one is
  missing.
- §4 Golden path — the expert step-by-step method: from dominant-risk
  identification through spike through walking-skeleton through final verdict.
- §5 What could go wrong — documented failure modes and root causes of failure,
  with severity; include the cognitive-bias failure modes (optimism bias,
  planning fallacy).
- §7 Proof spec — how experts judge a feasibility read-out; eyeball rubrics a
  non-developer Director can apply.

## Segments researched

Two Sonnet researchers ran in parallel, differentiated by focus:

1. **Method report** — the spike, PoC, walking skeleton, and verdict framework:
   Cagan/SVPG four risks; Kent Beck / Ward Cunningham spike origin; Alistair
   Cockburn walking skeleton; Amazon PRFAQ feasibility FAQ; ProdPad three-verdict
   model; Productboard five-dimension rubric.

2. **Quality/failure report** — failure modes, anti-patterns, root causes, and
   the eyeball rubric for a non-developer Director: Cagan on late engineer
   involvement; Teresa Torres on feasibility assumptions; bias literature
   (optimism bias, planning fallacy); named anti-patterns (PM-UX Discovery,
   validation-as-discovery); good/bad spike framing contrasts; spike-code
   disposal discipline.

## Claim discipline

tagged [S] and the load-bearing ones undergo a verification pass (verdicts in
`extracted-claims.md`). Confirmed material graduates to `grounding.md` with
caveats inline — inheriting the rung-1 pattern.
