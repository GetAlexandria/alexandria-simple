The Learning Plane

Why the library grew a third band, and what lives on it

Start with what Alexandria is to a product manager

It is two things at once: the place to store and share the plan, and the place to store and share performance information on how the plan is going.

The first half was already covered by two planes. The Strategy plane captures how we take company-level bets and pull them through into the product — three falsifiable wagers, the principles held while making them, and the product bets that refract each corporate wager into this product. The Product plane explains what the product is, has been, and will be — its contexts, entities, capabilities, and surfaces, card by card.

But what about storing and sharing the information that shapes the strategy and the product plan? That's the Learning plane. It captures the information that shapes our strategy and product on an ongoing basis — and when staffed with AI agents, it can help drive better real-time decisions about whether to change course or double down on a strategy or product path. A wager is only as honest as the channel that can prove it wrong; this plane is that channel, the one band where nothing may move a wager's confidence except a result.

Strategy stakes the wagers → Product embodies them; using it throws off evidence → Learning holds the proof ↺ and returns it upward to the wagers that raised it

The parts, row by row

A Learning plane's contexts are cut by the state its evidence is in — held, sought, or flowing — plus one that narrates the story those three tell together.

The keystone

Entity – Learning 1 card · the plane's front door

The plane's own story: the product's memory of what it has already figured out and its standing plan for what it must figure out next. It names the proof the whole plane exists to earn — the golden metric, Fair-Market Value Delivered, climbing on evidence rather than on hope — and narrates each shelf below in a sentence, the same move each shelf lead then makes for its own members.

Research

shelf lead: The Evidence We Hold 20 cards · evidence held

Evidence already settled. A lesson distilled from a body of prior work, a called test's result, or an observation noticed but not yet resolved — each filed as its own card rather than left as a footnote on the wager it informs, so a finding is as atomic and linkable as the bet it tests. Today: three corpora of past learning (high-reliability systems, system-builders, tools for thought), the internal lessons, and the first banked results.

Experiments

shelf lead: The Tests We Run 7 cards · evidence sought

The standing plan of bounded tests still to come — a question that ends by design. Every experiment writes its prediction before the run and commits its stop before it starts, so a test can't be quietly extended until it says what its owner hoped. Cheap disposable probes run beside instrumented full experiments with success measures and guardrails; both are graded for how rigorously they were run, an axis kept strictly apart from what they found.

Measurement

shelf lead: What We Watch 5 cards · evidence flowing

Quantities that never resolve to a verdict and simply report. The golden metric is the apex of the family — the fair-market value of the work the AI colleagues deliver, counted by real use and set against what they cost — and the per-wager measures that feed it tell the plane not just whether the product is winning but which wager to thank.

Arcs

shelf lead: The Stories We Tell 3 cards · the narrated stretches

The fourth shelf holds no evidence at all: an Arc is a named stretch of the product's story — intent going in, canon coming out — narrating the wager, the product work, and the evidence en route that carry one stretch, a release story told atomically. It is the keystone's move at the little-picture scale: a plan minus its execution, never a schedule. Today: Onboard Raven → Get Good Work in the air, The Colleague in the Meeting deliberately standing down.

What makes the cards trustworthy

Learning cards carry vitals in frontmatter that the catalog parses and the card drawer renders: an Experiment's kind, grade, state, expected outcome, and its pre-committed stop/guardrails; a Research card's origin and grade; a Measure's target and trend. Gates hold the shape by construction — every learning card must narrate its WHEN (the card's biography), every shelf lead must narrate and link every member (no orphans), and every wikilink lives inside a sentence as the key noun, so a reader flows through the story of the product experience while learning each modular term in context.
