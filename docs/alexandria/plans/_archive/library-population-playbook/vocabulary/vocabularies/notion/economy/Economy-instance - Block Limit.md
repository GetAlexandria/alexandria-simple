# Block Limit

## WHAT: Definition

_Stub — a Free-tier constraint that historically capped the total number of [[Entity - Block]]s a personal [[Entity - Workspace]] could contain. From roughly 2018 to 2021, the Free plan limited individual (personal) Workspaces to 1,000 Blocks total; exceeding this limit required upgrading to a paid plan. In May 2021, Notion removed the Block Limit for personal (single-member) Workspaces, making all Block creation unlimited on the Free tier for solo users. The Block Limit for multi-member Free Workspaces (where collaboration requires a plan upgrade) was addressed via the [[Economy-instance - Plan]] structure changes rather than a hard Block ceiling._

_The lifecycle of this constraint is instructive: the Block Limit was Notion's original monetization pressure on individual users. Its removal was a deliberate acquisition strategy — Notion announced that "Notion is now free for personal use" and the 1,000-Block ceiling was the primary barrier removed. The noun "Block Limit" survives in the vocabulary bank as a historical constraint with a known removal date, not as an active restriction. This is the Economy category's version of a soft deprecation: the constraint existed, shaped user behavior and pricing strategy for years, and was then deliberately lifted as a product move. Directors whose products have usage-based limits with planned sunset strategies should treat this lifecycle as a reference pattern._
