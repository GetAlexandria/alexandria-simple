# Retroactive Unlock

## WHAT: Definition

_Stub — the Metroidvania-defining pattern: acquiring a new Capability makes previously-visited-but-blocked areas traversable, turning the whole World into a retrospective unlock queue. The World does not change; the player's capabilities do, and those capabilities reach backward through the player's spatial memory. In Hollow Knight: the Mantis Claw makes tall vertical shafts climbable (Wall-Jumping retroactively unlocks every vertical surface the player has passed); the Crystal Heart makes long horizontal corridors super-dashable; the Monarch Wings makes chasms double-jumpable. Each acquisition is simultaneously a forward unlock (new areas) and a backward revisitation prompt (old areas worth returning to)._

_This pattern is the architectural identity of the Metroidvania genre — named from Metroid and Castlevania, both pioneers of the retroactive-unlock World design. Hollow Knight executes it with particular density and grace: the World is large enough that retroactive unlocks remain genuinely surprising rather than immediately obvious; the capabilities are calibrated so that the "aha" of a new capability cascades across remembered areas; and the Map supports the player's ability to track what they have not been able to access. Families.md names this as the "Retroactive Unlock" pattern with the Metroidvania citation; Hollow Knight is its reference implementation. Any director building a spatially-gated exploration game should treat this Lexicon's patterns/ folder as the expected output shape for that design investment._
