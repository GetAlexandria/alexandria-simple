# Research Vein 2: Business Operating Systems and Stage Frameworks

**Research question:** What recurring knowledge areas do business operating systems
require companies to maintain, and how do those requirements change with company
stage and type?

**Why this matters for Alexandria:** Alexandria currently models 22 product knowledge
areas. Expanding to corporate-level context libraries requires understanding the
operational knowledge layer that wraps around product knowledge.

---

## 1. EOS (Entrepreneurial Operating System)

**Source:** Gino Wickman, *Traction* (2007, revised 2012); EOS Worldwide.

**Target stage:** Companies with 10-250 employees. Typically $2M-$50M revenue.

### The Six Key Components (as Knowledge Areas)

#### 1. Vision — "Where are we going and how will we get there?"

- **Core Values** (3-7): Behavioral principles used as hiring/firing criteria.
- **Core Focus**: Purpose/cause/passion + niche (what you do better than anyone).
- **10-Year Target (BHAG)**: Single, measurable, long-range goal.
- **Marketing Strategy**: Target market, three uniques, proven process, guarantee.
- **3-Year Picture**: Revenue target, profit target, measurable characteristics.
- **1-Year Plan**: Revenue target, profit target, 3-7 company goals.
- **Quarterly Rocks**: 3-7 most important things this quarter.

The **Vision/Traction Organizer (V/TO)** captures all of the above on two pages.

#### 2. People — "Do we have the right people in the right seats?"

- **Accountability Chart**: Major functions, who owns each, 5 roles per seat.
- **People Analyzer**: Core values alignment + GWC (Get it, Want it, Capacity).
- **Right People / Right Seats assessment**: Culture fit AND role excellence.

#### 3. Data — "Are we managing by facts, not feelings?"

- **Scorecard**: 5-15 numbers with owners, weekly targets, and actuals.
- **Measurables per seat**: 1-3 success measures per person.

#### 4. Issues — "What's in the way?"

- **Issues List**: Prioritized obstacles at company, department, and personal levels.
- **IDS outcomes**: Resolution history (Identify, Discuss, Solve).

#### 5. Process — "What is our way of doing things?"

- **Core Processes**: 6-10 processes documented at the 80% level.
- **Followed By All (FBA)**: Agreement that everyone follows them.

#### 6. Traction — "Are we executing?"

- **Quarterly Rocks + status**: Binary on/off track per Rock.
- **To-do list status**: Short-term commitments from meetings.
- **Meeting pulse health**: Whether meeting cadence produces accountability.

### Where EOS Breaks Down

- Single Accountability Chart doesn't handle matrix orgs or multi-product.
- 3-7 company-wide Rocks too coarse for many independent teams.
- No governance layer — assumes single leadership team.
- Not justified below ~10 people; strains above ~250.

## 2. Scaling Up / Rockefeller Habits (Verne Harnish)

**Source:** Verne Harnish, *Scaling Up* (2014).

**Target stage:** $1M-$1B revenue. More sophisticated than EOS, designed to scale further.

### The Four Decisions Framework

#### 1. People

- **FACe (Functional Accountability Chart)**: Accountability with KPI linkage.
- **PACe (Process Accountability Chart)**: Cross-functional process ownership.
- **A-Player profile per role**: Behavioral and outcome markers.
- **Employee satisfaction data**: Start/stop/keep feedback.
- **Core values + behavioral evidence**.
- **Talent pipeline and bench strength**.

#### 2. Strategy

- **Core Customer profile**: Specific ideal customer with jobs-to-be-done.
- **Brand Promise**: Measurable guarantee to the core customer.
- **Brand Promise KPIs (KPIBS)**.
- **Profit per X**: The economic denominator.
- **BHAG**: 10-25 year aspirational target.
- **3-5 year key capabilities/moves**.
- **1-year critical numbers**.
- **Quarterly theme + critical number**.
- **Sandbox**: Where you play and where you DON'T play.
- **Key competitive advantages (3)**.
- **X-Factor**: The 10-100x advantage you're building.

The **One-Page Strategic Plan (OPSP)** captures all of the above.

#### 3. Execution

- **Quarterly priorities (Rocks)**: Company, department, and individual.
- **Critical Number**: Single most important metric this quarter.
- **Meeting rhythm intelligence**: Patterns across daily/weekly/monthly/quarterly cadences.
- **Organizational bottleneck map**.
- **Customer feedback patterns** (maintained weekly).
- **Employee feedback patterns**.
- **Process bottleneck identification**.

#### 4. Cash

- **Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)**: Sales cycle + production cycle + collection cycle.
- **Power of One analysis**: Impact of 1-unit lever moves on cash flow.
- **Operating cash flow vs. profit distinction**.
- **Working capital requirements by growth scenario**.

### Where Scaling Up Breaks Down

- OPSP assumes single business unit. Multi-product needs multiple OPSPs with unclear rollup.
- No board governance, investor relations, or compliance layer.
- Above ~500 employees, "everyone reads one OPSP" fails. Needs federation.

## 3. OKR Frameworks

### Google's OKR Approach (Doerr)

**Target stage:** Works from 5+ to 180,000+.

- **Company-level Objectives (3-5 per quarter)**: Qualitative, inspirational.
- **Key Results (2-5 per Objective)**: Quantitative, scored 0.0 to 1.0.
- **Team-level OKRs**: Aligned but not dictated.
- **Committed vs. Aspirational distinction**: Essential — treating aspirational as committed is a failure mode.
- **OKR lineage/alignment map**: Which team OKRs support which company OKRs?
- **Historical OKR scores**: Patterns reveal sandbagging or misalignment.
- **Current confidence levels**: Mid-quarter red/yellow/green.

### Radical Focus (Wodtke)

- **Single Objective per team per quarter** with 3 Key Results.
- **Health Metrics**: Things that must NOT break while pursuing the OKR.
- **Confidence scoring (weekly)**: Trajectory more informative than score.
- **Friday wins / learnings**: Knowledge about knowledge.

### Where OKRs Break Down

- Below ~5 people: overhead exceeds value.
- Without executive modeling: seen as surveillance.
- At high interdependency: alignment becomes quadratic.
- For maintenance/operations: OKRs favor change over continuity.

## 4. YC Startup School / YC Playbook

**Target stage:** Pre-seed through Series A. Deliberately minimal.

### Pre-PMF Knowledge

- **Problem definition** (one sentence)
- **User identity** (actual names, not personas)
- **Usage metrics** (the one metric that matters)
- **Growth rate** (week-over-week, 5-7% is "good")
- **Runway** (months until zero at current burn)
- **What users are telling you** (literal quotes)
- **Why users churn** (specific reasons)
- **Founder-market fit evidence**
- **Competitive landscape** (one paragraph)

### Post-PMF adds

- Unit economics (CAC, LTV, payback)
- Retention cohorts
- Hiring plan and culture definition
- Fundraising knowledge

### Where YC Breaks Down

- After ~50 employees: no coverage for departments, governance, compliance.
- For non-VC-track businesses.
- For regulated industries.

## 5. Strategyzer — Business Model Canvas + Value Proposition Canvas

**Target stage:** Exploration/validation and strategic inflection points.

### Nine Knowledge Blocks

1. **Customer Segments** 2. **Value Propositions** 3. **Channels**
4. **Customer Relationships** 5. **Revenue Streams** 6. **Key Resources**
7. **Key Activities** 8. **Key Partnerships** 9. **Cost Structure**

### Value Proposition Canvas

**Customer Profile:** Jobs, Pains, Gains.
**Value Map:** Products/Services, Pain Relievers, Gain Creators.
**Key artifact:** The fit assessment — falsifiable, not aspirational.

### Where Strategyzer Breaks Down

- Snapshot tool, not living dashboard.
- Captures WHAT not HOW WELL.
- No multi-business framework.
- Entirely customer-facing; nothing on internal operations.

## 6. Stage-Specific Knowledge Requirements

### Pre-Seed (1-3 people) — 5-10 knowledge artifacts
Problem statement, target customer, proposed solution, founder-market fit, market
size, competitive landscape, initial financial model.

### Seed (4-10 people) — 15-25 knowledge artifacts
Adds: PMF evidence, unit economics, growth rate, GTM strategy, use of funds,
team composition, runway, key risks.

### Series A (10-30 people) — 30-50 knowledge artifacts
Adds: Proven growth engine, cohort data, org plan, product roadmap, competitive
moat, board reporting, customer segmentation, sales pipeline, cash flow model,
culture docs, basic process docs.

### Growth / Series B+ (30-100 people) — 60-100+ knowledge artifacts
Adds: Departmental OKRs, management assessment, expansion strategy, platform
strategy, compliance, IP portfolio, partnership strategy, board governance, risk
register, succession planning, scenario analysis.

### Enterprise (500+ people) — hundreds of knowledge artifacts
Adds: Full governance, multi-year strategic plan, enterprise risk management,
compliance programs, ESG, M&A playbook, multi-market knowledge, vendor/supply
chain risk, internal audit, stakeholder frameworks.

## 7. Cross-Framework Synthesis

### Universal Knowledge Stack

**Layer 1: Existence Knowledge (day 1)**
- What problem we solve and for whom
- Why we will win
- How we make money

**Layer 2: Validation Knowledge (once you have users)**
- What users actually do and say
- Whether the economics work
- How much runway we have

**Layer 3: Direction Knowledge (once you have a team)**
- Where we're going (vision, BHAG, 3-year picture)
- What matters most right now (quarterly priorities)
- How we'll know we're on track (scorecard)
- What's in the way (issues list)

**Layer 4: People Knowledge (once you have >1 team)**
- Who is accountable for what
- What kind of people we are (core values, culture)
- Whether we have the right people in the right seats

**Layer 5: Execution Knowledge (once you have departments)**
- How we do things here (core processes)
- How goals cascade
- What customers and employees are telling us

**Layer 6: Governance Knowledge (once you have external stakeholders)**
- Board reporting and investor relations
- Compliance and regulatory requirements
- Risk management framework
- Financial planning and scenario analysis

**Layer 7: Enterprise Knowledge (at multi-market/multi-product scale)**
- Cross-unit coordination and resource allocation
- M&A integration playbooks
- Multi-market adaptation
- Succession and talent pipeline
- Stakeholder management across diverse constituencies

### Key Organizational Complexity Transitions

1. **Solo → Small Team (~5 people)**: Implicit → Explicit knowledge. The founder's
   head is no longer sufficient.

2. **Small Team → Departments (~25 people)**: Explicit → Structured knowledge.
   Things were written down but now need a system multiple teams can navigate.

3. **Departments → Divisions (~100-250 people)**: Structured → Federated knowledge.
   A single Accountability Chart no longer captures the org. Multiple coordinated
   systems needed.

4. **Divisions → Enterprise (~500+ people)**: Federated → Governed knowledge.
   The knowledge graph becomes a network of networks. Governance of knowledge
   itself becomes a first-class concern.

### Company Type Variations

| Dimension | Product Company | Services Company | Research Org | Marketplace |
|-----------|----------------|------------------|--------------|-------------|
| Heavy on | Customer knowledge, product roadmap, growth metrics | Delivery methodology, utilization, client relationships | Research agenda, publications, grants | Both-sides-of-market, network effects |
| Light on | Delivery methodology, utilization | Product roadmap, PMF metrics | Growth metrics, GTM | Single-segment focus |
| Unique | Competitive analysis, feature comparison | Client-specific institutional knowledge, methodology IP | Prior art, reproducibility, peer review | Liquidity metrics, trust/safety, platform governance |

## 8. Proposed Corporate Knowledge Domains

Based on the cross-framework synthesis:

1. **Identity & Direction** — Vision, mission, core values, BHAG, purpose
2. **Strategy** — Strategic bets, competitive position, target market, differentiation, sandbox
3. **Goals & Priorities** — Multi-horizon goals, Rocks/OKRs, critical numbers
4. **Customer Intelligence** — Segments, feedback patterns, retention, satisfaction
5. **People & Accountability** — Org structure, roles, culture criteria, talent pipeline
6. **Financial Model** — Unit economics, cash dynamics, revenue model, cost structure, runway
7. **Operations & Process** — Core processes, bottleneck map, delivery methodology
8. **Metrics & Measurement** — Scorecard, KPIs by function, leading vs. lagging, definitions
9. **Risk & Compliance** — Risk register, regulatory requirements, compliance status
10. **Governance** — Board structure, decision authority, policy framework, stakeholder communication
11. **Market & Competitive** — Market landscape, competitive intelligence, industry trends
12. **Partnerships & Ecosystem** — Key partnerships, vendor relationships, platform dependencies

### Configuration Dimensions

The wizard should configure based on:

- **Company stage**: Pre-seed, seed, Series A, growth, scale, enterprise
- **Company size** (employees): 1-5, 5-25, 25-100, 100-500, 500+
- **Company type**: Product, services, research, marketplace/platform, regulated
- **Governance complexity**: Solo founder, co-founders, board, public company
- **Regulatory environment**: Unregulated, lightly regulated, heavily regulated
