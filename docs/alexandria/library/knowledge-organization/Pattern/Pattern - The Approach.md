## WHAT

The current method Alexandria uses to organize a library: Domain-Driven
Design's structural grain crossed with a families taxonomy of
product-noun categories, read as two orthogonal classifying axes —
[[Entity - Type]] and [[Entity - Altitude]] — rather than one
contested list. It governs the whole system this area describes, and it
is not fixed: it is itself a wager, open to revision as evidence comes
back from using it.

## WHY

Method is what separates a wager from an intention:
[[Bet - Atomic, Agent-Readable Knowledge]] only becomes real through a
repeatable way of cutting a library into composable cards. Crossing the
structural grain with a product-noun taxonomy is that method's answer
to [[Principle - Legible Graph]] — a graph agents traverse that a
director can still walk.

## WHERE

Governs every other concept [[Entity - Knowledge Organization]]
describes — how the containers nest, how Type and Altitude classify,
and which buckets Type draws from. It does not sit beside those
concepts as a peer; it is the standing method that decided their shape.

## HOW

The Approach charters up to two Strategy-plane cards that stake its
underlying wagers: [[Principle - Legible Graph]], the rule that a
library's structure must stay navigable to a human and not just to
machines, and [[Bet - Atomic, Agent-Readable Knowledge]], the wager that
atomic, typed, linked cards let a colleague compose company knowledge
better than prose does. If either is revisited, the Approach changes
with it. What is learned about whether the Approach actually works
belongs to the Learning plane, which now exists and already holds the
evidence that tests it: [[Research - Is the Library Organization Method Working|whether the library's organization method is working]]
and [[Research - How We Chose the Library's Structure|how the library's structure was chosen]] are both carded there.
