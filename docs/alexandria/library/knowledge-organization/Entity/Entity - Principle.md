## WHAT

One of the fourteen families-category buckets: a normative rule the
company holds regardless of how any bet turns out. A principle
constrains rather than stakes a claim — it does not resolve to right or
wrong the way a bet does, it simply governs. Principle lives on the
Strategy plane.

## WHY

A standing rule earns its own card so a colleague can check what the
company holds constant apart from whichever wager is in play —
atomization applied to the company's own reasoning,
[[Bet - Atomic, Agent-Readable Knowledge]]. Naming a standing rule
once, rather than restating it inside every card it constrains, is what
keeps the graph a director reads free of drift between copies of the
same rule, [[Principle - Legible Graph]].

## WHERE

The bucket a card's [[Entity - Type]] carries when its subject is a
standing rule. Principle lives on the Strategy plane, where
[[Principle - Legible Graph]] is a worked example: a principle that
itself carries this bucket as its Type.

## HOW

Principle, one of the buckets in the [[Entity - Atomic Card Category]],
differs from its nearest neighbor, [[Entity - Bet]], by holding
regardless of outcome rather than staking a claim: a principle
constrains no matter how any bet turns out, while a bet is a claim that
evidence can move. Both grew out of
[[Entity - Rationale|the retired Rationale bucket]], the single
catch-all a choice's justification used to land in before it split into
a rule held no matter the outcome and a wager staked in advance.
