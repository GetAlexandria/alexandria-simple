## WHAT

The superstructure — the fourteen universal buckets (Bet, Principle,
Research, Experiment, Measure, Arc, Roles, Domains, Surfaces, Entities,
Capabilities, Mechanisms, Patterns, Economy) that most product companies'
nouns land in,
regardless of the specific product. Alexandria's metaschema for
product libraries: not in conflict with the card format — two
complementary layers of the same system, and the metaschema that
classifies every card in the library, this one included. Bet and
Principle are the two first-class buckets a choice's justification
splits into — a falsifiable wager and a normative rule — with
Rationale, the single catch-all bucket they grew out of, retired from
the live set.

## WHY

The fourteen buckets are the shared superstructure that lets
[[Bet - Atomic, Agent-Readable Knowledge|atomic, agent-readable knowledge]]
hold across a whole product rather than only inside one team's local
vocabulary — any card, whatever the product turns out to be, files
somewhere. Keeping the catalog small and closed serves
[[Principle - Legible Graph]] too: a director scanning for a kind of
noun faces fourteen familiar shelves, not an open-ended list.

## WHERE

The category catalog that classifies every card. Each bucket has its
own concept card, one level down, carrying its definition and how it
differs from its nearest neighbor.

## HOW

Each [[Entity - Atomic Card]] entry is filed under exactly one category
bucket — the categories are the superstructure, the card contract is
the format. Each bucket is also the value a card's [[Entity - Type]]
carries: the category and the Type field name the same classification,
read from two altitudes.
