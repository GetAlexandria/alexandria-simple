## WHAT

The wager: colleagues appear and speak in live audio and video meetings — the furthest reach of presence, a colleague joining the conversation in real time rather than only responding in text.

## WHY

Speaking inside a live meeting is the furthest a colleague's presence can reach, so this is as close to a final verdict as the wager gets: once a director accepts a colleague talking alongside them in real time, no quieter surface is left to prove presence actually works. A miss here isn't a silent subtraction but a visible one, and what the company learns is where a room's tolerance for AI participation actually sits — a lesson that costs more to absorb than pulling an unused feature would. Feasibility is no longer what's being tested, since the capability itself has already been shown to work; what's left to find out is reception.

## WHERE

A product-level refraction of the corporate bet that colleagues are the interaction layer — it charters up to [[Bet - Colleagues as the Interaction Layer]]. It is embodied by live meeting presence, a colleague speaking alongside the team on a call.

## HOW

If the wager holds, a colleague that can join and speak in a live meeting is the strongest possible proof that it is a team member, not a tool — presence at the moments that matter most. If it is wrong — a speaking agent alienates people who don't want to talk to an AI live — the mark is harder to walk back than pulling a silent feature, since it's a reputational moment rather than a quiet subtraction. Whether it can be built at all is no longer a live question — it has already been prototyped and demonstrated.

That feasibility side is already moved by [[Research - A Colleague Can Hold a Live Meeting Seat|the live-meeting result]]. The desirability side falls back for now to [[Experiment - Named Colleague Versus Invisible AI|the named-versus-invisible probe]], because no meeting-specific desirability experiment exists yet.
