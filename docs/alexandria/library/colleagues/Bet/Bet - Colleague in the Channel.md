## WHAT

The wager: colleagues are present in the team's own text channels — in the thread, part of the conversation — rather than behind a separate application a director has to go visit.

## WHY

Whether a colleague's presence has to be requested or can simply be assumed is what this wager tests: proving it out would mean the interaction layer needs no separate destination at all, arriving inside the conversation a team is already having rather than waiting to be visited. A miss would teach the opposite — that a channel is space people keep for each other — and push colleague presence back toward surfaces built and offered on purpose. Either way the company finds out cheaply, since the integration can simply be pulled without disturbing anything it sits alongside.

## WHERE

A product-level refraction of the corporate bet that colleagues are the interaction layer — it charters up to [[Bet - Colleagues as the Interaction Layer]]. It is embodied by the chat integration that lets a colleague join and speak in a team's existing channels, and it also touches the bet that the whole work environment should be immersive and present, not just traversible.

## HOW

If the wager holds, meeting the director in a channel they already live in is the purest form of colleagues as the interaction layer — no separate app to visit. If it is wrong — directors don't want agents in their team chat at all — the integration is cheap to pull without touching anything else.

At the shelf level, [[Experiment - Named Colleague Versus Invisible AI|the named-versus-invisible probe]] tests whether directors want AI as a managed colleague rather than hidden inside tools, and [[Measure - Fair-Market Value Delivered|fair-market value delivered]] is where that presence has to become useful work. A channel-specific desirability test is not carded yet.
