## WHAT

The wager: each colleague is a named individual with a persistent identity, role, and record — met like a specific teammate, not a generic capability.

## WHY

People return to individuals, not to capabilities — that is the working assumption behind giving each colleague a name and a history rather than a role and a rating. If it holds, trust accumulates around a specific name the way it would with any teammate, compounding every time the director works with them again. If it doesn't, the company has lost almost nothing: identity swaps out for a number and a color as easily as it went in, and nothing downstream was built to depend on a colleague having a face.

## WHERE

A product-level refraction of the corporate bet that colleagues are the interaction layer — it charters up to [[Bet - Colleagues as the Interaction Layer]]. It is embodied by the named agents and their roles — [[Role - AI Colleague]] and its named instances [[Role - Raven]] and [[Role - Damien]] — each carrying an identity that persists across every interaction.

## HOW

If the wager holds, a name and a persistent identity are what make a colleague feel like a specific teammate a director builds a working relationship with, not an interchangeable feature. If it is wrong — personification adds nothing over a number and a color — identities are trivial to swap for anonymized workers without disturbing anything else.

The nearest planned diagnostic is [[Experiment - Named Colleague Versus Invisible AI|the named-versus-invisible probe]], which asks whether directors choose a named managed colleague over AI hidden inside tools. If that shape earns use, [[Measure - Fair-Market Value Delivered|fair-market value delivered]] is where the value of the named colleague ultimately reads.
