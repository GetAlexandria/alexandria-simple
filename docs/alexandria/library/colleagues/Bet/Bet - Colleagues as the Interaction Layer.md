## WHAT

The wager: business adopts named, persistent AI colleagues as the primary way it interacts with AI — over AI kept hidden, embedded, or unnamed inside other tools. The colleague, not the feature, becomes the thing a team works with.

## WHY

Owning how a business interacts with AI at all is what every product bet on this shelf turns on, since none of them matter unless this wager holds. Confirming it would mean the company holds the primary relationship a business has with its AI — not a supplier of a feature tucked inside someone else's software, but the team a director actually directs. Losing it teaches that businesses prefer AI kept hidden and unnamed, narrowing the product's job from being colleagues to being invisible plumbing. Because this is the company's own core wager, walking it back would not trim a feature — it would change what Alexandria is.

## WHERE

A corporate bet — SocioTechnica's, not this product's — held here for now and marked to transfer to the Company Library once it exists. Alexandria is its current embodiment: colleagues are met as [[Entity - Coin|coins]] in the [[Surface - Tray|Tray]], they act through [[Entity - Play|plays]], and they show up in the team's own comms. It anchors the product refractions on this shelf, which charter up to it. Its north-star measure is human-colleague-hours transferred to full AI-colleague ownership.

## HOW

The interaction layer refracts into the product bets on this shelf: a team of [[Named Colleagues]], each met as a persistent individual and shown as [[The Coin as Abstract Token]] rather than a face or avatar, run from [[The Control-Panel Tray]]. They reach the director where the team already works — [[Colleague in the Channel]] in the team's own text threads and [[Colleague in the Meeting]] in live audio and video — and each takes ownership of work through [[The Play as Unit of Ownership]], executing it with the same [[Independent Execution]] a director would expect of a human peer, and drawing on [[The Deep Playbook]] whose growing depth is what makes a colleague worth working with.

The wager reads through [[Measure - Fair-Market Value Delivered|fair-market value delivered]], which watches whether colleague-owned work becomes real value, and through [[Measure - Needed But Undone Hours|needed-but-undone hours]], which watches whether wanted work still sits uncovered. [[Experiment - Named Colleague Versus Invisible AI|The named-versus-invisible probe]] asks whether people want a colleague shape at all, and [[Experiment - Ten-Director Library Pilot|the ten-director pilot]] is the first independent reading of whether a company-grown colleague earns continued use.

If the wager holds, the way a business runs shifts from operating software to directing colleagues — and whoever owns that interaction layer owns the relationship. If it is wrong — business prefers its AI hidden and unnamed — colleagues recede into automation, and Alexandria becomes invisible plumbing rather than a team you work with.
