## WHAT

The wager: a colleague is represented as an abstract coin — a token, not a face, avatar, or persona. The abstraction keeps a colleague legible as a working part of the team without borrowing the uncanny weight of a simulated person.

## WHY

How much likeness a working relationship actually needs sets the terms of this wager: whether an abstract token reads as respectfully spare or just cold. Proof would confirm that legibility, not resemblance, is what makes a colleague feel present and ownable inside the panel. A loss teaches the opposite — that directors want something closer to a face to bond with — but because the coin is only a thin skin over the same panel, that lesson is cheap to act on without touching anything beneath it.

## WHERE

A product-level refraction of the corporate bet that colleagues are the interaction layer — it charters up to [[Bet - Colleagues as the Interaction Layer]]. It is embodied by the [[Entity - Coin|coin]] a director meets each colleague as in the [[Surface - Tray|Tray]].

## HOW

If the wager holds, the abstract token is what lets a director treat AI as colleagues without the false intimacy of a human face — legible, ownable, unmistakably a tool that works like a teammate. If it is wrong — directors bond with faces and the token reads cold — the coin is reskinned as an avatar while the control panel it sits in is untouched, so being wrong here is cheap to walk back.

The current read is only proxy-grained: [[Experiment - Named Colleague Versus Invisible AI|the named-versus-invisible probe]] tests whether a managed named colleague beats hidden AI at all. The smaller coin-versus-avatar question remains a later representation test if the coarse colleague shape wins.
