## WHAT

The wager: a colleague takes ownership of work as a play — the unit by which work is assigned to a colleague, run consistently, and measured.

## WHY

This wager bets that a play is the unit that makes delegating to a colleague feel safe — the shape work gets assigned, run, and measured in. Holding up, everything already built around that assumption — how work is run, recorded, and reviewed — keeps paying off as the natural shape of ownership. Losing means people reject the play as the frame for what a colleague does, and because plays thread through so much of what already exists, unwinding that assumption is real rework rather than a quick substitution.

## WHERE

A product-level refraction of the corporate bet that colleagues are the interaction layer — it charters up to [[Bet - Colleagues as the Interaction Layer]]. It is embodied by the [[Entity - Play|plays]] a colleague runs — each run recorded as a [[Entity - Play Run|play run]] — and the [[Entity - Playbook|playbook]] they are drawn from, and it also touches the bet that great colleagues are grown from a centralized company substrate, since the play is where that substrate meets the director's actual work.

## HOW

If the wager holds, the play is what lets a director assign real work to a colleague the way they would to a human peer, and trust it will run consistently and be measurable. If it is wrong — people reject the play as a unit, whether the framing or the degree of autonomy it implies — the unit is reworkable, though because plays thread through the rest of the product, reworking it is not free.

Because the play is the unit by which work is assigned, run, and measured, [[Measure - Fair-Market Value Delivered|fair-market value delivered]] is the standing reading for whether play-owned work becomes real value. [[Experiment - Ten-Director Library Pilot|The ten-director pilot]] is where that unit first has to earn continued use from directors who are not us.
