## WHAT

The wager: depth is decisive — a large and growing volume of plays a colleague can run, not a small fixed set.

## WHY

Depth over a small curated set is a bet on one thing: whether a colleague's playbook keeps earning trust as it widens, or plateaus once it covers a handful of tricks. Holding up, the product's value compounds — every new play adds to what can be handed off, and depth becomes the reason a colleague keeps getting more useful over time. Losing teaches that directors only ever reach for a few trusted moves, in which case the playbook stays small and curated at little cost, since nothing else is built to depend on it growing large.

## WHERE

A product-level refraction of the corporate bet that colleagues are the interaction layer — it charters up to [[Bet - Colleagues as the Interaction Layer]]. It is embodied by the [[Entity - Playbook|playbook]] itself, the growing collection of plays a colleague draws on to take on more of a director's work over time.

## HOW

If the wager holds, depth of playbook is what lets a colleague keep absorbing more of a director's real work rather than plateauing at a handful of tricks. If it is wrong — directors only ever want a handful of hot-key plays — the playbook can stay small and curated, at little cost to anything built around it.

The current proof starts with [[Research - A Rulebook Turns Operator into Legislator|the rulebook lesson]], which grounds depth as safety infrastructure. It should read over time against [[Measure - Needed But Undone Hours|needed-but-undone hours]] and [[Measure - Fair-Market Value Delivered|fair-market value delivered]], because useful depth should both shrink uncovered ambition and raise delivered value.
