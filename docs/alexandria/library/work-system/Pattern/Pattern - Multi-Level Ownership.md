## WHAT

Ownership is assignable at many levels, and the levels genuinely
differ. The director's own worked example, now live: he could own a
System, delegate an improvement Project on it to Jess, while Raven is
in charge of the tasks that spawn from it. Three distinct slots — the
System's owner accountable for its health, an upgrade Project's owner
accountable for a bounded improvement, and a per-rule assignee working
the spawned cards — each fillable by a human or a colleague,
interchangeably.

## WHY

A mixed human-and-agent company cannot run on one accountability slot:
the person answerable for a system's health is routinely not the one
doing its chores, and the one improving it is often a third. Making
delegation structural — down to the individual pattern rule — is what
lets an agent own real infrastructure while human-only steps
(signatures, payments, filings) route to a person by design rather
than by memory.

## WHERE

On every level of the work model: [[Entity - Domain]] owners,
[[Entity - System]] and [[Entity - Work Project]] assignees, per-rule
delegation inside a System's pattern, and the assignee on every
[[Entity - Work Item]]. The map's Owner view regroups all work by
exactly these answers.

## HOW

Every assignment names a human or a colleague in one shared vocabulary,
and absence speaks too: an unowned Domain renders visibly unclaimed as
a demand signal — the Operations region's blank nameplate is a standing
hire requisition for the back-office colleague who doesn't exist yet.
In the live pilot the layers all differ: Raven owns LLC Administration,
its bookkeeping rule spawns to Raven, its tax and filing rules spawn to
the director, and its first upgrade Project — hiring a human
bookkeeper — is the loop noticing its own missing owner.
