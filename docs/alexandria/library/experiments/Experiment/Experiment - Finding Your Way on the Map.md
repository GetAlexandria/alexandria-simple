## WHAT

Give five people a real task and watch them find their way to it and act on it using the map, marking exactly where each one loses their footing — not whether they eventually get there, but where the path breaks down along the way.

## WHY

A visual, traversible environment only earns its keep if traversing it is actually easier than the alternative it replaces. If the map itself becomes an obstacle, the environment is quietly taxing the very people it exists to serve, and that tax has to be visible before the environment carries more weight in the product's story than it has earned. This is the friction check underneath the broader preference question the environment bet raises.

## WHERE

It tests [[Bet - A Visual, Traversible Work Environment]]; the specific friction it is watching for sits in [[Bet - Map-First Work Surface]], the bet that the map itself should be the primary way people orient and act, so what this probe finds speaks to both.

## WHEN

There is no past yet: N/A — no one has walked the map for this yet. In the present it is planned and shaped enough to run: a real task, five testers, no time limit of its own since finding the worst friction does not need a clock, only enough people. What is intended next is that it runs once and reports back a map of where people got lost, not a pass/fail score.

## HOW

The instrument is direct observation: watch a person attempt a real task on the map and mark, in place, wherever they hesitate, backtrack, or ask for help. There is no target score, because this is a probe, not a full experiment — the read is qualitative, a map of where the map itself loses people — and it stops at five testers, since a first pass needs enough volume to surface the worst friction, not enough to quantify how common it is.
