## WHAT

The experiments shelf holds the bounded tests the product runs to earn evidence
it does not yet have. What a bounded test is, and how it differs from a
quantity that simply reads without end, is defined in [[Entity - Experiment]].

## WHY

The shelf's tests exist to answer what the company's three wagers actually
need proven — whether a named colleague earns its keep at all, whether the
visual, traversible surface draws on its own rather than riding on the
colleagues beside it, whether a colleague grown from the company's own design
beats one assembled from parts — each built to come out either way rather
than confirm what its owner already believed.

## WHERE

Each experiment tests a company wager and runs inside the product that embodies
it, and what it leaves behind, once called, is a result on the research shelf.
The one designed test in hand is the
[[Experiment - Ten-Director Library Pilot|ten-director library pilot]], the plan
to put the library-building process in front of directors who are not us.

The subtler tests the strategy will need — chiefly isolating how much the
visual, traversible environment earns on its own rather than riding on the
colleagues beside it — are named in the plan and will land as their own cards
when they are designed, not linked before they exist.

## WHEN

There is no past yet: N/A — the shelf opens here with its lead and a single
planned test. In the present it holds that one experiment, still planned, its
prediction and stop not yet frozen. What is intended next is that each wager's
designed tests join it, so the shelf becomes the standing plan for what the
product must prove to earn its next releases.

## HOW

An experiment carries its weight in one of two classes. A probe is cheap,
disposable, and often run in parallel, with no metric required and only a
timebox or a count to stop it; a full experiment is instrumented, with a success
measure and guardrails that must not degrade regardless of the outcome. Both are
staged for how rigorously they were run — reported, demonstrated, piloted, or
at-scale — an axis kept strictly apart from what they found, so that a test run
at market breadth can still return a disappointing verdict. The prediction and
the stop freeze the moment a test starts, and that freezing is its
pre-registration; an instrument that turns out to be broken never reaches a
verdict, it cycles back and re-runs. Members group under the wager they
operationalize, tying the shelf back into the loop.

[[Experiment - Ten-Director Library Pilot]] is the shelf's one full experiment in hand, the piloted-grade flight of real, non-us directors that tests whether people want a managed colleague at all. Everything else here is a probe — cheaper, disposable, run ahead of or alongside the heavier tests — sorted the same way, by the wager each checks. [[Experiment - Named Colleague Versus Invisible AI]] runs on that same ten-director cohort just ahead of the pilot, forcing the coarsest choice — a colleague of one's own against AI folded invisibly into tools already in use — before any of the cohort's time is spent on the subtler question the pilot itself asks. [[Experiment - Spatial Surface Versus Plain List]] and [[Experiment - Finding Your Way on the Map]] both probe the visual, traversible environment riding underneath the colleagues: the first watches which surface people actually reach for when given the same task two ways, the second watches where a real map costs people their footing rather than whether they eventually arrive. [[Experiment - Isolating the Visual Environment's Draw]] is that same environment wager's fuller instrument, still unshaped and held behind the nearer-term colleague work, waiting on a method chosen from a portfolio the shelf can already cite. [[Experiment - Grown Colleague Versus Off-the-Shelf Agent]] probes the third wager directly, comparing a colleague grown from the company's own design against an off-the-shelf agent on the same real task, cold — the value-side companion to the feasibility work already running as the current build itself.
