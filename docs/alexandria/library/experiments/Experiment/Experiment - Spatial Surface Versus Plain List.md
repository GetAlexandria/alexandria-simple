## WHAT

Give the same task two ways — through a spatial, traversible surface and through a plain list of the same underlying work — and watch which one someone actually reaches for when both are equally available. That is the visual bet's value question made concrete and disposable: does the spatial surface earn real preference, or would a plain list do the same job just as well?

## WHY

This is the cheapest possible first read on whether the spatial surface is worth what it costs to build and keep working, before any heavier study is designed. A clear pull toward the surface is a directional signal worth deepening; an even split, or a pull toward the list, says the surface is not yet earning its keep on its own and the case for it needs to rest on something other than preference.

## WHERE

It tests [[Bet - A Visual, Traversible Work Environment]] and the product-level wager that work lives on a [[Bet - Map-First Work Surface|map-first work surface]], run as a small, disposable comparison rather than as the fuller instrumented look that bet's deeper question eventually needs.

## WHEN

There is no past yet: N/A — no one has been given the choice yet. In the present it is planned, shaped enough to run — the task, the two surfaces, and the stop are already set. What is intended next is that it runs once, over a handful of testers, and reports back a directional read rather than a settled verdict.

## HOW

The instrument is behavioral, not a preference survey: watch which surface a tester actually picks when given the same task both ways, rather than asking which they say they would prefer. No success threshold is set, because a probe carries no metric, only a stop — five testers or one week, whichever comes first, since this is a first, disposable look meant to find a direction quickly, not to quantify how strong it is. A clear, repeated pull toward the spatial surface is worth carrying into a fuller, better-instrumented look; an even split means the surface's case has to be made some other way.
