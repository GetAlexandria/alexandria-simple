## WHAT

Give ten directors who are not us the process for building their own product
library, together with a Raven grown from what they document, and the colleague
that produces will be useful enough — real enough — that they actually build with
it, keep using it afterward, and would pay for it, rather than trying it once and
setting it aside. That is the company-grown wager restated as the hypothesis it
secretly is: that documenting your product, and potentially your strategy and
go-to-market plan, progressively unlocks a colleague with more fidelity than a
generic pile of skills or a standalone agent built apart from your company. If
directors will not build, or build and do not return, or return but would not pay,
the wager that a colleague grown from your own company earns real use is in
trouble, and this is the cohort where we find that out.

## WHY

This is the first real test of the company-grown wager on people who are not its
makers. Our own use cannot settle it — we are the least representative, most
invested sample there is, and everything we have seen so far is only demonstrated
in strength for exactly that reason. Real, independent directors are what carries
the wager from something shown on ourselves to something piloted in the world, and
this pilot is also the product's first real reading of value delivered to people
who did not build it. It rides on, but does not settle, the separate wager that
people want a managed AI colleague at all: if directors rejected AI colleagues
outright nothing here would matter, but that is a coarse, loud failure mode this
cohort cannot cleanly test either way, since most of these trialers are close to
solopreneurs with little of the integration friction a managed colleague is
supposed to resolve for larger companies. What this pilot can actually move is the
fidelity claim: whether a colleague grown out of a director's own documented
company clears a real usefulness bar.

## WHERE

It tests the wager that a
[[Bet - Colleagues Grown from Company Design|colleague grown from a company's own design]]
earns real use, and rides on, without settling, the wager that
[[Bet - Colleagues as the Interaction Layer|colleagues are the interaction layer]].

It runs inside the product's library-building process and the Raven that process
powers — the director builds the library, and Raven is the colleague whose fidelity
the build is meant to raise. What it leaves behind, once called, is a settled
reading on whether real directors value a company-grown colleague enough to keep it
and pay for it.

## WHEN

There is no past yet: N/A — no past yet. The nearest prior evidence is our own
dogfooding, which is only demonstrated in strength because the sample is us. In the
present it is planned and waiting on two things — Alexandria Prime being finished,
and a first director being ready to begin. The intended future is that when the
first director starts, the prediction and the stopping rule freeze, and that moment
is the pre-registration; the pilot then runs to whichever comes first of ten
directors or ninety days, and reports a reading that adjusts the company-grown
wager's confidence.

## HOW

The instrument is behavioral, not opinion, held to the bar we have kept all along:
we watch what each director does, not what they say. There are four readings.
Whether they complete a library at all, and how far they carry it. Whether they keep
using Raven after the build, unprompted by us, at thirty days. The fair-market value
their Raven actually delivered across the window, valued the way the golden metric
defines it, so that this pilot doubles as that metric's first reading on directors
who are not us. And whether they will commit money at about one hundred dollars per
AI employee each month, which is the revealed-preference signal that outranks any
stated intention. Feedback and feature requests are kept as the qualitative floor —
they shape the next turn, they do not count as proof.

The prediction is written before the run, so a surprise is the signal rather than a
story told afterward: roughly six of ten finish a first library, about four of ten
still use Raven unprompted at thirty days, two or three say yes to paying. The
prediction and the stopping rule are committed the day the first director starts and
are not revised after.

The stopping rule is a count of ten directors or a window of ninety days, whichever
arrives first, the ninety days chosen because it is the window in which the colleague
wager is front and center. Two guardrails can halt it earlier regardless of how it is
going: if running it starves the work of finishing Alexandria Prime it pauses, since
Prime is the precondition for everything after; and it never puts a director in front
of something that would embarrass us with them.

A confirming reading looks like most directors building a library, a real share of
them still living in Raven a month later, and a couple willing to pay — evidence, at
piloted strength, that a library-grown colleague earns its place. A denying reading
looks like directors who will not build, or build and drift away, or would never pay.
Because this is piloted rather than proven at scale, and the sample is ten, a denial
moves the company-grown wager's confidence without settling it, and it names what
would have to change before we would run it again.
