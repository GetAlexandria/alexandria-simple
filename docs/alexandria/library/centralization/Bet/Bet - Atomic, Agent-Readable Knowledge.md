## WHAT

The wager: knowledge is atomized into typed cards in a linked graph — a representation colleagues read and compose better than narrative documents.

## WHY

Winning this wager buys colleagues that compose the company's knowledge with precision — reading a graph of typed facts instead of guessing across long documents as the library grows past what any one person could hold in mind. Losing it teaches something narrower and cheaper: that the cost of atomizing everything wasn't earned, and that plain prose or retrieval would have served colleagues just as well. Because the fallback can be added alongside what already exists, the company finds this out without having to tear the library down first.

## WHERE

A product-level refraction of the corporate bet that great colleagues are grown from a centralized company substrate — it charters up to [[Bet - Colleagues Grown from Company Design]]. It is embodied by the [[Entity - Atomic Card|atomic cards]] themselves and the graph of links between them, organized the way [[Entity - Knowledge Organization|knowledge organization]] enshrines.

## HOW

If the wager holds, atomic and linked knowledge is what lets a colleague compose company understanding precisely, rather than searching and guessing across long documents. If it is wrong — colleagues do just as well on plain prose or retrieval over documents — the atomic form isn't worth its cost; the fallback is reworkable, prose or retrieval added alongside, though re-representing the library itself would still be a costly undertaking.

The nearest value diagnostic is [[Experiment - Grown Colleague Versus Off-the-Shelf Agent|the grown-versus-off-the-shelf probe]], because the atomized library has to show up in better work from a grown colleague. Its current grounding is [[Research - Represent Knowledge with Models, Not Symbols|the model-not-symbol lesson]] and [[Research - How We Chose the Library's Structure|the library-structure result]], which say why this representation was chosen and how it has already been tried on the company's own knowledge.
