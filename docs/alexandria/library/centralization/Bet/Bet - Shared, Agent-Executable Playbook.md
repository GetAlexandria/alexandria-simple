## WHAT

The wager: process is centralized in one shared playbook every colleague draws on — the source of consistency — rather than living as per-agent scripts.

## WHY

If this wager holds, the payoff is consistency that compounds — one playbook every colleague runs and improves together, rather than a pile of per-agent scripts nobody maintains in common. Losing it teaches that centralizing process bought nothing colleagues couldn't already get running their own scripts on their own. That finding is cheap to act on, since the design itself stays reworkable rather than foundational — the company can walk it back without a deeper rebuild.

## WHERE

A product-level refraction of the corporate bet that great colleagues are grown from a centralized company substrate — it charters up to [[Bet - Colleagues Grown from Company Design]]. It is embodied by the shared [[Entity - Playbook|playbook]] and the [[Entity - Play|plays]] every colleague runs from it, and it also touches the bet that a colleague takes ownership of work as a play, since the playbook is what makes that ownership consistent across every colleague.

## HOW

If the wager holds, one shared playbook is what keeps every colleague's process consistent and improvable in one place, rather than fragmenting into scripts nobody maintains together. If it is wrong — per-agent process works just as well — the shared playbook's advantage disappears, though the design itself is reworkable rather than foundational.

The head-to-head read is [[Experiment - Grown Colleague Versus Off-the-Shelf Agent|the grown-versus-off-the-shelf probe]], which asks whether the shared substrate produces better work than a bolted-together agent. The reason to believe shared process matters is grounded by [[Research - A Rulebook Turns Operator into Legislator|the rulebook lesson]], where pre-made decisions turn process depth into calm execution rather than improvisation.
