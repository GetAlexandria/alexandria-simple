## WHAT

Every high-reliability domain in this corpus defines its critical roles apart from the
specific person filling them at any given moment. A named operational role — a console, a
watch station, a duty officer — carries the responsibilities, the authority, and the
procedures, and whoever is sitting in that seat inherits all of it; the role does not
belong to the individual, and the individual is never the whole of what the role is. That
separation is what lets these organizations rotate people through high-stakes duty without
the duty itself ever being unowned.

## WHY

This lesson matters because it validates naming AI colleagues as persistent roles rather
than as interchangeable capability: a colleague is not a feature invoked when needed, it is
a defined role that persists and can be handed off, staffed, or extended, in the same way a
watch station persists across every person who has ever stood it. It is evidence, from
domains where getting this wrong has real consequences, that role-and-person separation is
what makes accountable, tempo-holding work possible at all.

## WHERE

This lesson grounds the wager that each colleague is a
[[Bet - Named Colleagues|named individual]] with a persistent identity and role,
distinct from any one instance of the underlying model running it.

It also supports the broader wager that
[[Bet - Colleagues as the Interaction Layer|colleagues are the interaction layer]]
a business works through, since a persistent, role-bearing colleague is what that
interaction layer is claiming to offer.

## WHEN

This card's past is N/A — it opens here at its founding layer, drawing on operational
practice built up over decades across several fields. What stands now is a reported-grade
reading: the separation of role from person is well attested where humans rotate through
defined duty stations, but not yet tested against whether the same separation reads the
same way when the "person" filling an AI colleague's role is a model that can itself change
or improve over time. What is intended next is that the product's own named colleagues,
once used daily, give this lesson a demonstrated reading.

## HOW

The evidence is a desk-research synthesis of role-and-person separation as practiced across
operational domains built around human duty rotation, not a measurement of how directors
experience the product's own named colleagues. It establishes that the separation holds
and matters where the "person" changes but the role does not; it does not yet establish
whether it holds the same way in reverse — where the role's name and identity stay fixed
while the underlying model behind it can itself be upgraded or replaced, a case none of the
ten domains in this corpus actually faced.
