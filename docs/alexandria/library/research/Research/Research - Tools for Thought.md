## WHAT

This corpus is the tradition of tools built to extend what a human mind can hold and
manipulate, rather than tools that merely automate a task. It argues that a mind
represents the world in stages — first through action, then through image, and only
last through abstract symbol — and that skipping the image stage in favor of pure text
does not speed understanding, it removes a step the mind actually needs. Four lessons
follow from this tradition: that the image-based stage is a required step text-only
interaction bypasses, that working memory can hold only a handful of things at once, that
knowledge is better represented as models and simulations than as prose, and that the
value of a body of knowledge lives in the connections between its parts, not in the parts
read alone.

## WHY

Holding this evidence buys the product a reason, grounded in how minds actually work
rather than in taste, for building a visual, structured library instead of a purely
textual one. It explains why the product's other corpus of proven building patterns
succeeds cognitively rather than only aesthetically: the two corpora converge on the same
answer from different directions, one from what has been shipped, the other from what
minds require, and together they make a much stronger case than either alone.
