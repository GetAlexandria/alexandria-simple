## WHAT

A colleague can join a live audio and video meeting and speak in it, in real time,
alongside the rest of the team, and this has now actually been built and shown working
rather than only proposed. It is the strongest form of presence a colleague can offer:
not a message read later, but a seat at the table while the conversation is happening.

## WHY

This result matters because it retires a question that used to sit entirely open: whether
a colleague speaking live in a meeting was buildable at all. It no longer is an open
question — it has been demonstrated on ourselves. What remains open is a separate
question this result does not touch: whether people actually want a colleague speaking
live in their meetings, as opposed to whether one can.

## WHERE

This result retires the feasibility risk on the wager that a
[[Bet - Colleague in the Meeting|colleague appears and speaks in live meetings]],
without moving that wager's desirability risk one way or the other.

It also stands as a concrete instance of the broader wager that
[[Bet - Colleagues as the Interaction Layer|colleagues are the interaction layer]]
a business works through, since a colleague present in a live meeting is about as
far as that interaction layer can reach.

## WHEN

What happened is that a colleague was built and shown speaking live in a real meeting,
proving the capability exists. What stands now is that the feasibility question is
settled, while the meeting-focused initiative this result came out of is being wound down
in favor of finishing the product's own library first — the evidence this result banked
stays held regardless of the initiative's own pace. What is intended next is that this
result waits to be drawn on again once the desirability question — whether people actually
want a colleague speaking live, not just whether one can — becomes the more urgent
question to answer.

## HOW

The evidence is a working demonstration, not a survey or a stated intention: the colleague
was actually built and actually spoke in a live setting, which is do-level evidence rather
than say-level. It was demonstrated on the team's own constructed instance rather than on
an independent audience unconnected to the people who built it, so it settles feasibility
without speaking at all to whether people outside the team would want this in their own
meetings — that is a piloted-grade question this result does not attempt to answer.
