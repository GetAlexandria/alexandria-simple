## WHAT

Knowledge is better held as models and simulations a person can manipulate than as
symbolic text a person can only read. A block of prose describing how a company's parts
relate asks a reader to reconstruct the relationships themselves, every time, from words
alone; a model that already holds those relationships as structure lets a person see and
move through them directly. The difference is not style, it is what has to happen inside a
reader's head to get from the description to the understanding.

## WHY

This lesson matters because it is the argument for why company knowledge belongs in typed,
linked cards rather than long documents: a library built as a structured model, not a pile
of prose, is doing part of the understanding work up front so a person or a colleague does
not have to redo it every time they read it. It is the clearest cognitive-science backing
this product has for choosing atomized, linked representation over narrative documentation.

## WHERE

This lesson grounds the wager that a company's knowledge should be
[[Bet - Atomic, Agent-Readable Knowledge|atomized into typed, linked cards]]
rather than held as narrative prose.

It also supports the broader wager that great
[[Bet - Colleagues Grown from Company Design|colleagues are grown from a deliberately designed company substrate]],
since a model-based library is what makes that substrate something a colleague
can actually compose from rather than merely search.

## WHEN

This card's past is N/A — it opens here at its founding layer, drawing on a line of
thinking about knowledge representation that predates this shelf. What stands now is a
reported-grade theory synthesis, not yet tested against how well a colleague or a director
actually composes understanding from the product's own atomized library. What is intended
next is that the library, once populated and used by both directors and colleagues, gives
this lesson a demonstrated reading: whether typed, linked cards genuinely outperform prose
at the moment someone needs to understand or act.

## HOW

The evidence is a desk-research synthesis of internal thinking about representing
knowledge as models rather than as narrative text, not a measurement taken on this
product's own colleagues or directors. It establishes the theoretical case for structured,
model-based representation over prose; it does not yet establish how much of the benefit
survives once the modeling itself takes real, ongoing effort to build and keep current, or
whether a colleague reading atomized cards actually composes better answers than one
reading well-written prose would. That comparison is what using the product's own library
at real scale would settle.
