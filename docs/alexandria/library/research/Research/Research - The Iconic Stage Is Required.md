## WHAT

A mind builds understanding through three stages in order: first by acting on the world,
then by holding an image of it, and only after that by handling it as abstract symbol.
Text-only interaction — the kind a purely conversational AI offers — asks a person to jump
straight to the symbolic stage and skips the image-based stage the mind actually passes
through on the way there. That missing stage is not a nicety cognitive development can do
without; it is a load-bearing step, and forcing symbol without image is forcing a shortcut
the mind was not built to take.

## WHY

This lesson matters because it turns "make it visual" from a design preference into a
cognitive-science claim: the image-based, visual-spatial stage is a required intermediate a
person's understanding passes through, not decoration layered on top of the real, textual
work. If it holds, an AI colleague experienced purely through chat is asking a director to
skip a stage their own understanding depends on, and a visual, traversible surface is
supplying that missing stage rather than merely making things prettier.

## WHERE

This lesson grounds, at the level of cognitive science rather than proven software,
the same wager the building-pattern corpus grounds by precedent — that the work
environment must be a
[[Bet - A Visual, Traversible Work Environment|visual, traversible work environment]].

Read alongside the system-builder corpus's lesson that people own a world by constructing
it, this lesson supplies the reason that construction works: it is what lets a person pass
through the image-based stage a text-only interface would have skipped.

## HOW

The evidence is a desk-research synthesis of a cognitive-development theory applied to
software interaction, not a measurement of this product's own users. It establishes that
the image-based stage is a genuine, well-studied step in how understanding develops
generally; it does not yet establish that a director specifically experiences that missing
stage as a felt gap when using text-only AI, or that supplying it visually measurably
improves how well they understand and manage their company. That is what watching real
directors work the product's visual surface would confirm or deny.
