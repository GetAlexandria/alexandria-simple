## WHAT

A person comes to place, orient themselves in, and remember a large world because they
built it from a seed, piece by piece — not because they were handed a finished map of it.
The city-and-civilization genre bears this out again and again: a player can recall the
shape of a city or an empire years later because they laid every district and border
themselves, in an order they chose, and that act of construction is what fixes the world
in memory in a way that studying someone else's finished version never does. Constructing
even a rough diagram of a system teaches a person more about it than reading a polished
one someone else drew.

## WHY

This lesson matters because it answers a question the product cannot afford to get wrong:
whether a director should be handed a pre-built picture of their company or should build
that picture themselves. If building beats reading, then the effortful, slower path of
having a director construct their own company's library is not friction to be designed
away — it is the mechanism that makes the resulting picture theirs, legible, and durable
in a way a pre-filled template could never be.

## WHERE

This lesson grounds the wager that the work environment must be a
[[Bet - A Visual, Traversible Work Environment|visual, traversible work environment]],
because owning a constructed world is what that visual, traversible environment is
ultimately for.

It is also the lesson that most directly justifies asking a director to build their own
company's library from the ground up rather than starting from a filled-in template —
the same construction-first thesis the product's own onboarding is staking itself on.

## WHEN

This card's past is N/A — it opens here at its founding layer, carried forward from a
software genre with a long history but newly read into this shelf. What stands now is a
reported-grade reading, not yet tested against how a real director experiences building
their own company's library inside this product. What is intended next is that the
product's own construction-first onboarding gives this lesson a demonstrated reading, once
directors have actually built with it.

## HOW

The evidence is a desk-research synthesis, not a behavioral test: a pattern read
consistently across a software genre and the internal notes written about it, rather than
something observed on a real director inside this product yet. It is a say-adjacent form
of do-evidence — what these systems were built to make players do, and what players
report remembering afterward — read from outside the product rather than measured on it.
It does not yet establish that the same effect holds for a director building a company's
library rather than a city or an empire; that is exactly what construction-first
onboarding, once it runs, would confirm or deny.
