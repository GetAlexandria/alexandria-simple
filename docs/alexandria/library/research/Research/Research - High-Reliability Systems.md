## WHAT

This corpus is the field guide to how organizations that cannot afford to fail actually run
high-tempo, high-stakes work — control towers, ships at sea, operating rooms, mission
control, power grids, and the rest. Read across ten very different domains, the same
substrate keeps reappearing underneath all of them: an append-only record of what actually
happened, a shared vocabulary for the things that record talks about, roles that are
defined apart from whichever person is filling them at the moment, and a grammar for how
time and sequence are recorded. Six lessons follow from reading these domains together as
one convergent body rather than as separate case studies.

## WHY

Holding this evidence buys the product proof, from domains that cannot afford to fail, that
a company's central substrate is not a nice-to-have layer but the precondition for
everything built on top of it. The pattern recurring identically across ten unrelated
fields is stronger evidence than any one of them alone could offer, and it is why the
product's own foundations — a recorded history, a shared vocabulary of things, roles
separable from people, and a grammar for time — were treated as a starting requirement
rather than an eventual refinement.

## HOW

The corpus is a single lead over six member lessons, sitting at the aggregate grain because
a body of research opens, is drawn from, and reopens as new material joins it, rather than
being ranked as a value alongside its own members. Most lessons are cross-cutting claims
distilled across the ten domains rather than observations tied to one, each grounded by the
domain evidence cited on its own card. Two of the six carry a demonstrated reading rather
than a merely reported one, because they describe a pattern the product's own build already
embodies, not only one read out of other fields.
