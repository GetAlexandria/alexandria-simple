## WHAT

The wager: plays and playbooks are seen and traversed as visual objects, not only executed under the hood.

## WHY

Making plays and playbooks visible pays off in trust: a director who can see and move through how work actually runs, not only confirm that it finished, is a director who can vouch for the process rather than take it on faith. A miss here is cheap to absorb — the visualization falls away and the plays keep running exactly as before, only unseen again. What the company learns from either outcome is whether visibility itself carries value, separate from the execution it depicts.

## WHERE

A product-level refraction of the corporate bet that the work environment must be visual and traversible — it charters up to [[Bet - A Visual, Traversible Work Environment]]. It is embodied by the view of the [[Entity - Playbook|playbook]] a director sees and moves through — still nascent — and it also touches the bet that colleagues own work as plays, since this is what makes that ownership visible rather than hidden.

## HOW

If the wager holds, seeing plays and playbooks as visual objects is what lets a director understand and trust how work actually runs, not just that it ran. If it is wrong — seeing them adds little over just running them — the visualization can be dropped at little cost to the plays and playbooks underneath it.

The nearest current read is [[Experiment - Spatial Surface Versus Plain List|the spatial-versus-list probe]], because this wager is a specific case of choosing a visual work surface over a plain one. [[Research - The Map Is the Command Center|The map-as-command-center lesson]] grounds the claim that seeing and acting on work in the same place carries value, while a playbook-view-specific probe remains uncarded.
