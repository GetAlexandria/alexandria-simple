## WHAT

The wager: work and the key decisions inside it live on a single visualized map the director moves through — a map-first work surface, rather than a set of separate lists and boards.

## WHY

Winning this wager buys directors a single place to hold their work and its decisions, replacing the constant switching between disconnected lists and boards with one surface they move through. Losing it costs little: the product learns that a map added polish without navigability, and the surface reverts to plain lists and boards without disturbing anything underneath. Between those outcomes sits the real test — whether a spatial view is something directors actually reach for, or simply admire.

## WHERE

A product-level refraction of the corporate bet that the work environment must be visual and traversible — it charters up to [[Bet - A Visual, Traversible Work Environment]]. It is embodied by the map surface a director navigates as their primary view of work in progress — still to build, with the [[Mechanism - Canvas|canvas]] its nearest built ancestor.

## HOW

If the wager holds, a map-first surface is what lets a director hold all of their work and its key decisions in one navigable place, rather than switching between separate lists and boards. If it is wrong — directors don't want a spatial map, or a map that isn't genuinely navigable adds nothing over a pretty diagram — the surface is cheap to undo, reverting to plain lists and boards.

The first reads are [[Experiment - Spatial Surface Versus Plain List|the spatial-versus-list probe]], which watches which surface people reach for, and [[Experiment - Finding Your Way on the Map|the map-finding probe]], which watches where navigation breaks. [[Research - The Map Is the Command Center|The map-as-command-center lesson]] is the precedent this wager is trying to make true in the product.
